# Changelog

## Unreleased — 1.5.0 (pre-release 1.5.0-dev.2)

- **7-day and 30-day charts read 5-minute summaries (performance).** A 30-day chart read every reading in its window (about 43,000 for a source collected each minute) in one synchronous query that held the event loop for 23–28 ms on the development Mac, and so delayed collection and every other request. `history.sqlite` now keeps one summary row per source and 5-minute slot (`history_summaries`: the slot's last success, last failure, last pause marker, first failure, success count, first and last reading, the reading before it and the longest silence inside it per point), written in the same transaction as the reading, a pause marker, recycling on a full disk, pruning and the removal of a source. Charts of 7 days or more are built from those rows: one indexed range per bucket instead of a scan and sort of every reading, then the readings of the chosen points. Their buckets are now whole 5-minute slots from a slot boundary (7 days: 35-minute buckets, 30 days: 145 minutes; the window can start up to 5 minutes earlier), for the reading path as well, at most 299 buckets; shorter charts are unchanged. A property test compares both paths on random histories (failures, markers, pauses, rewrites through `INSERT OR REPLACE`, readings out of order or in the future, pruning, removed sources) over 7, 30 and 90 days, and a budget test requires a 30-day chart of a month of minute readings to be at least 5 times faster than from the readings (p95 at most 10 ms with `TEST_REQUIRE=capacity`); measured 3.8 ms against 23.3 ms. The table is additive: 1.4.x and earlier 1.5 builds ignore it, and readings they write (after a rollback, a restore or a tool) are found at the next start, which checks every source's summaries against its readings in transactions of about 5 ms that yield between them and rewrites what differs; until a source is checked, and while pruning or removing a source, its charts read its readings. The check's first step of a source is at most an hour of slots (fewer for a source with more than about 1,000 readings an hour), and a step that finds nothing skips to the next reading, so a stray reading at an extreme time costs one step. A clean shutdown records a fingerprint of each checked source's readings (`history_summary_marks`: their count, first and last time, and sums of their times, successes and pause markers, plus the source's summary count; at most a second of such work, the sources left out keeping their old marks), and a start checks slot by slot only the sources whose fingerprint changed since: any write to a source's readings by a release or tool that keeps no summaries, a moved, replaced or deleted reading included, changes it, and after a crash or a write by another connection while Uptime Goblin ran the sources are checked as before. Measured with 40 sources of 30 days of minute readings on the development Mac: a start's check of unchanged data takes 0.16–0.20 s instead of 0.89 s, and the shutdown that records the fingerprints about 0.17–0.22 s (the first start after an upgrade still writes every summary, about 3 s). A summary read that fails once for a passing reason (the database busy or locked, out of memory, an I/O error) sends only that chart to the readings; the same failure three reads in a row, or any other (a damaged page, a missing table, an SQLite without `concat_ws`), stops the summaries until the next start, and the chart is read from the readings instead of failing. The summaries take about 110 bytes per source per 5 minutes (about 1 MB per source per 30 days) and are never backed up or transferred. Recording a reading costs about 0.1 ms more.
- **ntfy and Gotify notification channels.** Alerts, recoveries and Uptime Goblin's own notices can now also be pushed to [ntfy](docs/notifications.md#ntfy) (`https://ntfy.sh` or a self-hosted server: topic, optional access token or user name and password, the priority of an alert and of a recovery, tags) and [Gotify](docs/notifications.md#gotify) (server and application token, the two priorities), each set up and tested under **Configure → Notifications → Push notifications**, with an optional click-through to Uptime Goblin's address. They use the durable queue like email and the webhook (saved first, retried with a stable `X-Goblin-Delivery`, in order per alert, in the delivery history), pause on a `401` or `403` until the settings are saved, a test is accepted or **Resume** is pressed, and tell the other channels when they pause; while their settings cannot be read, their notifications wait and are queued once they can (alert changes stay in the history meanwhile, with the email and webhook rows they put in memory when `control.sqlite` cannot be written either, and any number of them leaves newer alerts' notifications going; Uptime Goblin's own notices wait in memory, at most 1,000, and past that the oldest are not sent to ntfy and Gotify, counted in the log). Tokens and passwords are write-only: sealed with the encryption key in `control.sqlite`, answered only as `{"set": true|false}`, and recorded in the audit log (`channel.settings`, `channel.test`) only as set, changed or cleared, as is the ntfy topic. Their servers follow the collection destinations' address rule (loopback only with `GOBLIN_ALLOW_LOOPBACK=1`; link-local, metadata, multicast and reserved addresses never), checked at connect time; `https:` is required unless the server is on this computer or **Allow plain HTTP** is switched on; redirects are never followed and requests, answers and time are bounded. New routes: `GET` and `PATCH /api/notifications/channels/<id>/settings` and `POST /api/notifications/channels/<id>/test` for `ntfy` and `gotify` ([HTTP API](docs/architecture.md#http-api)). `GET /api/notifications`, the dashboard's notification fields and the delivery history now list four channels.
- **Import from Uptime Kuma.** **Configure → Sources → Import from Uptime Kuma** reads an Uptime Kuma 1.x JSON backup (Settings → Backup → Export), previews what it would add, change (an Uptime Goblin Website check of the same name) and skip, and saves nothing until **Import**: then every added and changed Website check is saved in one configuration revision, only if the configuration is still the previewed one (409 otherwise, and the page shows the new preview), and recorded as one audit entry (new action `sources.import`, listed under **Sources** in the Audit log's Kind filter). HTTP(S) and keyword monitors become Website checks with their name, address, `GET`/`HEAD` method, interval (clamped to 15–3600 seconds), accepted statuses and Uptime Kuma group; keywords, followed redirects and ignored TLS errors are listed as not imitated, and every other monitor type is skipped with its reason. Credentials (basic auth, headers, bodies, client certificates, OAuth, credentials in an address) and notifications are never imported, and a monitor that sent them is marked **Needs credentials re-entered** (a secret whatever its authentication method, the fields of the method it used, or a basic-auth user name when no method is saved, as older Uptime Kuma releases sent it then; the method alone and the OAuth defaults Uptime Kuma saves on every monitor mark nothing). A monitor checked through a proxy is noted, as Uptime Goblin connects directly. A changed Website check keeps its Uptime Goblin group unless the monitor names an Uptime Kuma group, and the change runs the same follow-up steps as editing the source. The browser reduces the backup before sending it (credentials replaced by `redacted`, addresses cleaned, at most 8 MB read, 1,000 monitors), so no monitor credential leaves it. API: `POST /api/import/uptime-kuma/preview` and `POST /api/import/uptime-kuma` (`If-Match` required), both needing the `config:import` permission and the write headers, with the 150,000-byte request limit; a backup that is not a readable 1.x backup (another version, a field named `__proto__`, `constructor` or `prototype` in the backup or a monitor, two monitors with one id) gets 400. No `goblin-admin` command. The credential-parameter rules of destination links moved unchanged into `public/shared/url-credentials.js`, so the browser cleans addresses with the server's rules. [Importing from Uptime Kuma](docs/configuration.md#importing-from-uptime-kuma); `test/kuma-import.test.mjs`, `test/kuma-import-routes.test.mjs` and `test/kuma-import-ui.test.mjs` cover the parser, the routes and the page.
- **Orbit is now Uptime Goblin.** The server's runtime names change, and every old name keeps working in 1.5 and 1.6 (removed in 1.7): settings are read as `GOBLIN_*`, each falling back to its `ORBIT_*` name while unset, with one notice at every start that names the settings to rename; both names set to different values refuse the start (exit status 78) and name both, never the values. Scripts send `X-Goblin-Request: 1` (`X-Orbit-Request: 1` still passes; a present `X-Goblin-Request` decides alone), and answers carry `X-Goblin-Build` and `X-Goblin-Version` beside the old names. Webhooks carry `X-Goblin-Delivery` and `X-Goblin-Signature`, and the same values under `X-Orbit-Delivery` and `X-Orbit-Signature`; their `User-Agent` is `Uptime-Goblin`. The session cookie is `goblin_session` (`__Host-goblin_session` over HTTPS): an `orbit_session` cookie signs in while no new cookie comes, and is replaced on that answer by the same session under the new name, a wall display's included. The admin command is `lib/cli/goblin-admin.mjs`; `lib/cli/orbit-admin.mjs` runs it with the same arguments and exit status after a deprecation line. The data-directory lock is `goblin.lock`, taken together with `orbit.lock`, so an Orbit release can never run on the same data directory at the same time. Messages name the new paths and account (`/opt/uptime-goblin`, `/var/lib/uptime-goblin`, `/etc/uptime-goblin.env`, `/etc/uptime-goblin/secret.key`, `uptime-goblin.service`, user `uptime-goblin`); the installer, the systemd unit and the Docker files use them too (next entry), and an older release is still called Orbit where a message names it. Stored data, audit actions, signing key formats, the legacy webhook body's `source: 'orbit'`, the Basic realm and the Compose secret's and systemd credential's file names are unchanged.
- **The installer and the container move to the Uptime Goblin names.** The native installer is `deploy/install.sh` (`deploy/install-lxc.sh` runs it with the same arguments and exit status, after a deprecation line, until 1.7) and installs `uptime-goblin.service`, running as the `uptime-goblin` account from `/opt/uptime-goblin` on `/var/lib/uptime-goblin`, with `/etc/uptime-goblin.env` and `/etc/uptime-goblin/secret.key`; a new install writes only the new names. On a host with the Orbit layout (`orbit.service`, `/opt/orbit`, `/var/lib/orbit`, `/etc/orbit.env`, `/etc/orbit/secret.key`, the account `orbit`) its checks read the files where they are (and name them so), and the upgrade then moves the layout in one logged step, in place of the service stop: it stops `orbit.service`; renames `/var/lib/orbit`; writes `/etc/uptime-goblin.env` from `/etc/orbit.env` (each `ORBIT_` name written as its `GOBLIN_` one, the values as they were, a path in `/etc/orbit` or `/var/lib/orbit` written as its new path; mode `0600`) and keeps the old file as `/etc/orbit.env.migrated`; renames `/etc/orbit` (modes kept), the account and its group (`usermod -l`, `groupmod -n`, so every file keeps its owner) and `orbit.service.d`; disables `orbit.service` and keeps its unit as `orbit.service.migrated`; and renames `/opt/orbit`, which the upgrade keeps as `/opt/uptime-goblin.previous`. Every move is a rename on one file system: a mount point, a symbolic link, a drop-in of `orbit.service` outside `/etc/systemd/system/orbit.service.d` (or one that names an Orbit path), an account `orbit` whose home is not `/opt/orbit`, or both an `ORBIT_` name and its `GOBLIN_` name with different values, is refused before anything changes, and so is a host with parts of both layouts. A failed upgrade restores the Orbit layout exactly, `orbit.service` enabled and started again as it was, and checks that the previous release serves. The migration keeps a record (`/var/lib/uptime-goblin.migrating`) until the upgrade is verified: a run cut short (killed, or the power lost) is undone by the next run before its checks, which then migrates again. The installer holds `/run/orbit-install.lock` beside its own `/run/uptime-goblin-install.lock`, so an Orbit installer cannot run at the same time; its checks take the `ORBIT_` names in `/etc/orbit.env`, a drop-in or systemd's own environment as Uptime Goblin takes them. The Docker image is `uptime-goblin:local`, its data directory `/var/lib/uptime-goblin` (`GOBLIN_DATA_DIR`); a volume still mounted only at `/app/data`, where the image kept its data, is used there with a notice at every start, and one beside data at the new path refuses the start (exit status 78). The Compose service is `uptime-goblin` (the first `docker compose up -d --remove-orphans` removes the `orbit` container, which otherwise keeps port 4317); the volume keeps its name, `orbit-data`, and so its data, and `compose.yaml` reads each setting from `.env` under its `GOBLIN_` name, else its `ORBIT_` one (an empty `GOBLIN_` line counts as set, except for `GOBLIN_BIND_ADDRESS` and `GOBLIN_ALLOWED_HOSTS`), and passes each `ORBIT_` line as `GOBLIN_COMPOSE_LEGACY_<name>` too, which Uptime Goblin only compares: both names with different values refuse the start (exit status 78) and an old name gets the start notice, as natively. `deploy/install.sh --restore-orbit-layout` goes back to the Orbit release an upgrade from the Orbit layout kept, after the upgrade worked: it refuses, changing nothing, unless that release, `/etc/orbit.env.migrated` and `orbit.service.migrated` are there and nothing is in the way, then undoes the migration with its own rollback (the same record, so a run cut short is finished by the next one), keeps the settings Uptime Goblin ran with as `/etc/uptime-goblin.env.kept` and checks that `orbit.service` serves. Restoring the Orbit layout also removes the temporary copy of the new settings file and of the migration record that a run killed at the wrong moment left. The Compose secret's file (`orbit_secret_key`) and the systemd credential (`orbit-secret-key`) keep their names. The release archive is `uptime-goblin-<version>.tar.gz`; its signing namespace and identity are unchanged.
- The installation guide now says what a failed upgrade from the Orbit layout keeps on a host that had no encryption key: the key the upgrade wrote before the move, moved back to `/etc/orbit/secret.key` and named at the end of `/etc/orbit.env`, since settings the failed start sealed open only with it. No Uptime Goblin path is left, so the next run migrates again (with that key) instead of refusing a host with parts of both layouts; a new installer test covers it.
- **The systemd unit is active only once Uptime Goblin serves.** `deploy/uptime-goblin.service` was `Type=simple`, so systemd called it active as soon as the process started, before it listened. It is now `Type=notify`: Uptime Goblin sends READY=1 once it listens and has written `run/status.json`, through `systemd-notify --ready` (Node cannot send a unix datagram; absolute path, no shell, only `NOTIFY_SOCKET` in its environment), hence `NotifyAccess=all` ([security](docs/security.md)). `TimeoutStartSec=infinity` keeps a long start (a migration, an index build) from being killed, as with `Type=simple`. `systemctl start` and `restart` now return once it serves, and fail for a start that exits before (a refusal, exit 78, included). The installer starts the unit with `--no-block` and verifies the process as before, and its rollback makes the unit `Type=simple` for the restored release, since an earlier release never reports ready (the next upgrade writes `Type=notify` again); a release swapped back by hand needs `Type=simple` too ([installation](docs/installation.md#updates-and-rollback)). Tested under real systemd 257 (Debian 13): install, restart, upgrade, rollback, a restart after an unexpected exit 0, a refusal not retried, reboot, and the real 1.4.3 upgrade.
- **The documentation and the web app say Uptime Goblin.** The guides, README, examples, the footer and download names (`uptime-goblin-<source>-<range>.csv`, `uptime-goblin-audit.*`) use the new name, and the backup and restore commands use the new paths and the Compose service `uptime-goblin`. Installation has a [renamed from Orbit](docs/installation.md#renamed-from-orbit) section with what changed, what keeps its old name and an upgrade checklist. The rollback to 1.4.3 after an upgrade that worked is documented and tested for both installs ([rolling back](docs/installation.md#rolling-back-15-to-143)): natively `deploy/install.sh --restore-orbit-layout` (next entry), under Docker Compose a `compose.override.yaml` that runs the kept `orbit-homelab:1.4.3` image in the 1.5 `compose.yaml` with the volume at `/app/data` and the five settings 1.4.3 reads under their `ORBIT_` names. After a rollback the kept 1.5 `goblin-admin` names the Orbit layout's unit, account and key file (`orbit.service`, `sudo -u orbit`, `/etc/orbit/secret.key`) in its command lines for a data directory at `/var/lib/orbit`, and a refusal that finds Orbit 1.4.x running says `sudo systemctl stop orbit`; `goblin-admin status` labels a line about an Orbit release "Orbit:". A repository test fails on "Orbit" in shipped or documented text outside the compatibility code, the pre-1.5 entries and the names kept on purpose.
- **Nodemailer 10.6.1 (security).** Replaces 10.0.10, which has five published advisories (address parsing that ends a quoted local part, comment or group early, and quadratic backtracking in SMTP AUTH detection; fixed by 10.1.4) and a later recipient-handling fix (10.4.1). No settings change.
- Webhook signing no longer adopts retired revealable secrets or permits unsigned delivery. Migrated signing keys stay held until Resume; newer read-only settings preserve saved holds and report a neutral compatibility refusal. Pending storage repairs stay visibly unsaved, and route/fake-service regressions cover safe Resume ordering.
- Export-script migration: authenticated downloads from `GET /api/config/export` and `GET /api/audit/export` require `X-Goblin-Request: 1`, including legacy Basic clients on HTTPS and loopback. Headerless export requests receive 403 `cross_origin`; add the header to existing 1.4.x scripts. [Complete interactive and scheduled-job examples](docs/authentication.md#update-export-scripts-from-14x) keep credentials out of command arguments and exports in private files. Authentication, permissions, Basic defaults and other request behavior are unchanged.
- **Deflate bodies are decoded as they are read (security).** A source's `Content-Encoding: deflate` answer (zlib-wrapped or raw) was inflated a whole received chunk at a time, ahead of the collector's reader, so one 64 KB chunk of a compression bomb could take about 64 MB of memory before the 16 MB response limit applied. The decoder now follows the reader, as the gzip and brotli decoders do, and fails the body if more than 4 MB of its output is ever left unread. Accepted encodings and decoded bodies are unchanged.

- **Collection errors fail closed, and stored note codes are matched exactly (security).** The error text an admin sees for a failed collection (dashboard and test-service answer) no longer keeps an address or credential word because the text also says check, permissions or authentication: only the fixed texts of collector errors (`CollectionError`) pass through, and any other text naming an address, token, password, authorization or session ID becomes the generic message. A configured key or password is also redacted when percent-encoded, form-encoded or base64 (including a Basic `user:password` credential). Four fixed Proxmox, Cosmos and AMP texts that name a token are now collector errors, so they read as before, and a sign-in that returns an unusable session token now says so instead of the generic message. Wall displays rebuild a stored note only from a code Uptime Goblin defines, so a stored code such as `constructor` is dropped instead of reaching the display as an object. `test/collection-error.test.mjs` and `test/collectors-notes.test.mjs` cover both.

- **Public source hygiene (engineering).** The source tree can be published without the project's private history or internal coordination material. The internal handoff document is removed, `AGENTS.md` is public contributor and agent guidance without private paths, personal names in documentation, comments and tests are role-neutral (a test login is a fictional placeholder), and the gate catalog names the change-19 decision `GATE-CONTRACT-v5-change19-decision.txt` (its pinned sha256, the decision document's bytes, is unchanged; the catalog file's own sha256 changes with the label). Comments, test titles, documentation and the catalog's status text also name the private reviewers, coordinators, decision files and message ids of the development process only by role (the security review, an independent review, the maintainer's decision), with every finding id and rule kept; the catalog file's sha256 changes again with its status text. `scripts/security-entries.mjs` keeps its wording, since the catalog pins its bytes as the record-pack validator profile. The tests that start from a release tag or development milestone (1.4.2, 1.4.3, 1.5.0-dev.0, 1.5.0-dev.1) read the project's history from a private input that `TEST_HISTORY` names (a repository or bundle, the milestones' commit ids, the commit the 1.4.2 fixture was built from and the reviewed rewrites of recorded commits; `test/upgrade/history.mjs`), never from the checkout, and follow the new `TEST_REQUIRE=history`: without the input they skip, and with `history` required a missing or wrong input fails. The 1.4.2 fixture no longer records a commit id (its pin changes; its runtime files do not), `test/fixtures/authorship-rewrite.json` is removed, and the milestones are named by version. Public tests check the provenance logic on a synthetic repository, tag and rewrite list: the exact commit, a listed rewrite with the same tree (also from a bundle), a missing mapping, a wrong commit, a wrong tree, changed files and a missing or malformed input. Public CI no longer fetches history; [the private historical qualification](docs/engineering-quality.md#the-private-historical-qualification) says how to run it. The security gate does not pass `TEST_HISTORY` to its test stages yet, so there those tests skip and block.

- **Focused regression checks (engineering).** Review the dispatch logger and synthetic image-inspect stand-ins narrowly in the Docker classifier, retaining counterexamples for real launches without limits and other executable paths. Installer documentation checks now follow the named proxy upgrade note and the existing/effective proxy preflight. The authority-root test makes its non-repository fixture explicit when the temporary directory is inside a checkout, keeps the exact refusal, and checks that a valid nested root still binds its own repository.

The 1.5 branch, with every fix of the maintenance candidate below. This section describes source changes; it is not a deployment record.

- **Not releasable as 1.5.0 yet.** This is pre-release 1.5.0-dev.2. Still to come before the release: failed-sign-in counts and trusted clients kept across restarts (a restart clears them now), device cookies, a `goblin-admin` command that signs out every session (`session_seq`: the next request of each gets 401), installer v2 (staged releases, signature verification, the full preflight, a slow migration left to finish while it makes progress, code-only rollback with an audit anchor, the snapshot restored only on a refusal, adopting a mixed layout, refusing installs older than 1.4.2), the SMTP password and source credentials sealed with the encryption key (so far they are plain text in `config.json` and in every snapshot), log levels for Uptime Goblin's own output (Off, Error, Warning, Informational, Debug, Trace), and an approved review of the audit log.
- **Breaking: a password is always required (security).** Uptime Goblin no longer starts without an admin password: with neither `auth.json` nor `GOBLIN_PASSWORD` it refuses to start (exit status 78) before writing anything, on loopback as on any other address, with "Uptime Goblin needs an admin password, and none is set: …" and how to set one (`GOBLIN_PASSWORD` in `/etc/uptime-goblin.env`, where the native installer sets one, or in `.env` for `npm start` and Docker Compose; or restore `auth.json`). It never generates one. The passwordless *local preview* of 1.4.x and earlier 1.5 pre-releases is gone: it answered every request from the Uptime Goblin computer, with or without credentials, as the admin, so any process there that could reach Uptime Goblin's port (another user's, a local service's) had full admin access. Now no request is served without a credential except the sign-in page and its files, the sign-in itself and `/health`: a header-less request from the Uptime Goblin computer gets 401 `signin_required` like any other. Gone with it: the "Local preview" chip and account page, the start line "Sign-in: none — local preview…", the two start refusals that allowed it on loopback only ("Set GOBLIN_PASSWORD before listening on a network interface", "…before exposing Uptime Goblin through a reverse proxy or a non-loopback hostname"), the 403 "Set GOBLIN_PASSWORD before exposing Uptime Goblin through a reverse proxy." for proxied requests, the error code `signin_unavailable`, and the 403 "Set an Uptime Goblin login password before sending test email." **If you ran Uptime Goblin without a password, set `GOBLIN_PASSWORD` before upgrading**: the first start with it makes it the admin password. The native installer checks this before it changes anything: where `/var/lib/uptime-goblin` has no `auth.json` and systemd would give Uptime Goblin no `GOBLIN_PASSWORD`, or an empty one (from `/etc/uptime-goblin.env` or a drop-in, worked out as for `HOST` and `PORT`), the install or upgrade ends with "This release of Uptime Goblin would refuse to start (exit status 78): Uptime Goblin needs an admin password, and none is set: …", changing nothing, instead of stopping Uptime Goblin, failing to start it and rolling back (or, installing over an existing `/etc/uptime-goblin.env`, leaving Uptime Goblin down). A `GOBLIN_PASSWORD` that Uptime Goblin refuses (under 12 characters, only spaces, or over 1024 bytes) stops it the same way, with `This release of Uptime Goblin would refuse to start (exit status 78) with "GOBLIN_PASSWORD must be 12 to 1024 characters and not only spaces. …": …`. So does an `auth.json` that Uptime Goblin's start would refuse, read as root with the new release's own reader, the one that start reads it with (a symbolic link, a directory, a FIFO, an empty or cut-off file, `{}` or another invalid record, or one over 8192 bytes), whatever `GOBLIN_PASSWORD` is, since Uptime Goblin ignores `GOBLIN_PASSWORD` while `auth.json` exists: `This release of Uptime Goblin would refuse to start (exit status 78) with "Saved authentication could not be loaded. …": …`. An `auth.json` that the `uptime-goblin` account, which Uptime Goblin runs as, cannot read (one copied in from a backup with `sudo cp` is `root:root`, mode `0600`) stops it too, once the installer has given that account the data directory and before it stops Uptime Goblin or changes its code or settings, with "/var/lib/uptime-goblin/auth.json cannot be read by the uptime-goblin account, which Uptime Goblin runs as, …" and the `chown` that fixes it: every start of Uptime Goblin would fail with it (exit status 1). A new install, which writes a random `GOBLIN_PASSWORD` into a new `/etc/uptime-goblin.env`, passes unless a drop-in removes or empties it. `GET /api/system` keeps `authenticationRequired` (now always `true`) and `authentication.passwordChangeAvailable` (always `true`). Audit entries that earlier pre-releases recorded for the local preview still read as before.
- **Breaking: plain-HTTP sign-in is never turned on where the network can see it unless you choose it (security).** The native installer no longer adds `GOBLIN_INSECURE_HTTP_SESSIONS=1` by itself (1.5 builds so far added it for an install that listens on the network without a trusted proxy, and added it again after it was removed). Such an install or upgrade, one from 1.4.x included, now stops before changing anything ("Uptime Goblin would listen on … with no reverse proxy it trusts (GOBLIN_TRUST_PROXY), so browsers and scripts would sign in over plain HTTP, …") unless systemd would already give Uptime Goblin a yes or no there (from `/etc/uptime-goblin.env`, kept as it is, a drop-in or its own environment, the trusted proxy worked out the same way), and says how to put Uptime Goblin behind an HTTPS proxy (`HOST=127.0.0.1` and `--trust-proxy loopback`, or `--trust-proxy <proxy address>`) or how to choose plain HTTP: the new option `--allow-insecure-http` adds the line with a comment naming the date, who ran the installer and that passwords cross the network in clear, and warns (where systemd would give Uptime Goblin a `0`, from the file, a drop-in or its own environment, it is not applied, and the installer says so and names where a `0` that is not in the file comes from: a line added to the file would override a drop-in's `Environment=`). The `GOBLIN_INSECURE_HTTP_SESSIONS=1` that installers of earlier 1.5 pre-releases added by themselves, with the comment line "# Added by the Uptime Goblin 1.5 installer: keeps plain-HTTP sign-in working as in 1.4.x. Set it to 0 once Uptime Goblin is served over HTTPS." just before it, is no one's choice and decides nothing: every install whose `/etc/uptime-goblin.env` holds it stops before changing anything, on loopback and behind a trusted proxy too (a client that reaches Uptime Goblin on the network directly, or a proxy on the Uptime Goblin host that relays plain HTTP, would still sign in over plain HTTP under it), and names the line and the three ways to decide: set it to `0`, keep it by removing that comment or putting one of your own in its place, or run the installer again with `--allow-insecure-http`, which keeps the line and puts its own comment in place of that one (builds so far let such an install through on loopback or behind a trusted proxy with a warning, and the line stayed in force). Docker Compose no longer passes `1` while `.env` does not set it, but `compose-default`, which Uptime Goblin reads by where the port is published (`GOBLIN_BIND_ADDRESS`, passed in as `GOBLIN_PUBLISHED_ADDRESS`): on for a loopback address (the default `127.0.0.1`, which only the Docker host reaches), off on another address when `GOBLIN_TRUST_PROXY` names a proxy, and otherwise a refusal to start (exit status 78, "Docker Compose publishes Uptime Goblin on … (GOBLIN_BIND_ADDRESS), which is not a loopback address, with no reverse proxy Uptime Goblin trusts (GOBLIN_TRUST_PROXY), …") that names the three choices in `.env`: `GOBLIN_TRUST_PROXY` behind an HTTPS proxy, `GOBLIN_INSECURE_HTTP_SESSIONS=1` or `=0`. `1`, `0` and the other yes/no values decide as before; outside Compose (no `GOBLIN_PUBLISHED_ADDRESS`) `compose-default` is a value that is neither yes nor no (a start warning, and off). Legacy Basic requires the same opt-in on the plain-HTTP transport (`GOBLIN_INSECURE_HTTP_SESSIONS=1`, or Compose's `compose-default` while the port is published on loopback): without it a script's Basic there gets 403 `https_required`, the answer a sign-in gets, before its password is checked (not counted as a failed sign-in, no Basic challenge on plain HTTP, counted under **Scripts with a password**, one log line an hour); with it, Basic there is checked, a browser's remembered 1.4.x Basic login included wherever the browser reaches Uptime Goblin at a plain-HTTP address on the network: browsers send no `Sec-Fetch-Site` there, so a request a browser makes there on its own is taken for a script's (the entry on browser requests below). They do send it to `localhost`, `127.x` and `[::1]`, so a current browser at `http://localhost:4317`, where Compose's default publishes the port, is never checked. Loopback and HTTPS through a trusted proxy are unchanged. While plain-HTTP sign-in is allowed on an address the network reaches, the start warning also names the password scripts send with Basic (while it is on), and every signed-in page keeps the plain-HTTP banner. While it is allowed on loopback only, the start warns too when `GOBLIN_TRUST_PROXY` names a proxy or `GOBLIN_ALLOWED_HOSTS` a host that is not loopback: a proxy there that relays sign-ins over plain HTTP is accepted under the same opt-in. `GOBLIN_LEGACY_BASIC` with a value that is neither yes nor no (`of`, `disabled`) now refuses the start (exit status 78, "GOBLIN_LEGACY_BASIC must be 1 or 0 (true, yes or on; false, no or off), or empty for the default; it is …") before anything is written, instead of a warning and the default, which is on for upgraded installs; the native installer checks it in `/etc/uptime-goblin.env`, and as systemd would give it to Uptime Goblin, before an upgrade changes anything.
- **Sign-in page and sessions** replace the browser's HTTP Basic prompt for the built-in `admin`: a sign-in page, **Keep me signed in on this device** (12 hours idle for every session; at most 7 days, or 30 days with the box ticked), **Sign out** in a menu under your name, and **Configure → Account** (the former Security page) with the password change and your sessions (**Sign out**, **Sign out other devices**). A session that ends while you edit opens a **Sign in again** dialog that keeps your unsaved changes and finishes the save once. See [signing in](docs/authentication.md).
- Session cookies are `HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Strict` and, over HTTPS, `__Host-` and `Secure`; only their SHA-256 is stored. Uptime Goblin issues them over HTTPS through a trusted proxy, to a browser on the Uptime Goblin computer itself, or over plain HTTP only with `GOBLIN_INSECURE_HTTP_SESSIONS=1` or Docker Compose's default (`compose-default`) while it publishes the port on loopback (with a warning on the sign-in page and a banner: the password then crosses the network in clear too, at every sign-in and password change). An `http://` page whose sign-in Uptime Goblin would refuse (`https_required`, `proxy_proto_mismatch`) shows the reason without a password form, and an open page shows it without **Sign in** (a **Sign in again** dialog left open closes): Uptime Goblin could refuse such a sign-in only after the password had crossed the network. A proxy on the Uptime Goblin computer that sends no forwarding headers is plain HTTP, not the computer itself, and the sign-in page tells it apart by its own address. Each session is bound to the way it signed in: a cookie issued over plain HTTP never works as the secure one, and turning `GOBLIN_INSECURE_HTTP_SESSIONS` off (`0`) signs those sessions out at the next start. Browsers send Uptime Goblin's cookie to every port of its host name, so give Uptime Goblin a host name of its own. Every refusal names its fix on the page and in the log. Sign-in and every change with a session need an `Origin` matching the address or a `GOBLIN_ALLOWED_HOSTS` name.
- The admin password moves into the new control database (`control.sqlite`, with snapshots before each migration) and every change made in Uptime Goblin is written back to `auth.json`, so rolling back to 1.4.x keeps the current password (once `auth.json` has been written: while it cannot be, a banner says so, and 1.4.x would use the previous password). A change on a disk that fills just after `auth.json` was written still answers with your new sign-in cookie, since the change went through; the next start notes it. Uptime Goblin notes a new `auth.json` only once it is on disk, so a power loss just after a change (on btrfs, as Synology's Docker volumes use, up to 30 seconds after) cannot bring the previous password back and sign everyone out. A password changed outside Uptime Goblin (under 1.4.x, or a new `auth.json` or `GOBLIN_PASSWORD`) is imported at the next start and signs every session out; so does restoring a snapshot, and so does restoring a backup of the data directory (with `tar`, `cp` or a volume copy: Uptime Goblin notices that `control.sqlite` is another file, which would otherwise bring back every session of the backup's time, those signed out since included). A rollback of a file-system snapshot (a Proxmox LXC or VM snapshot, ZFS, LVM) leaves the same file, which Uptime Goblin cannot tell from a restart: after one, sign in and use **Sign out other devices**. A start with neither `auth.json` nor `GOBLIN_PASSWORD` is refused (exit status 78): a password is always required (above).
- Browser requests (with `Sec-Fetch-Site` or `X-Goblin-Client`) never have their `Authorization` checked or counted, so a browser remembering 1.4.x's Basic login locks nobody out. Browsers send no `Sec-Fetch-Site` to a plain-HTTP address on the network (they do to `localhost`, `127.x` and `[::1]`), so where Uptime Goblin accepts Basic there (with the plain-HTTP opt-in, or through a proxy on the Uptime Goblin computer that sends no forwarding headers and keeps a loopback Host) a request a browser makes on its own (a typed address, a link, an image) is checked like a script's, and one that is not signed in gets the Basic challenge scripts get, so the browser can still show its Basic prompt there. **The configuration and audit exports with Basic need `X-Goblin-Request: 1` on every transport (security; breaking for 1.4.x scripts that export without it)**, as with a session cookie (403 `cross_origin` otherwise, before the export is built or its audit entry written), so another site's image or link that carries a browser's remembered Basic login cannot make Uptime Goblin build exports and add their audit entries wherever the browser sends no `Sec-Fetch-Site`: at a plain-HTTP address on the network (not `localhost`, `127.x` or `[::1]`), Uptime Goblin's own or that of a proxy on the Uptime Goblin computer that sends no forwarding headers and keeps a loopback Host (which Uptime Goblin takes for the computer's own), or over HTTPS from a browser too old to send it. A script that exports sends that header, as it does with every change, over HTTPS and from the Uptime Goblin computer itself too; 1.4.x needed none on an export. Scripts keep HTTP Basic on upgraded installs (`GOBLIN_LEGACY_BASIC`; off on new installs, also after `control.sqlite` is removed: `created-by.json` in the data directory records that 1.5 created it; on the plain-HTTP transport only with `GOBLIN_INSECURE_HTTP_SESSIONS=1` or Docker Compose's default while it publishes the port on loopback, but with neither, and no warning in the log, through a proxy on the Uptime Goblin computer that sends no forwarding headers and keeps a loopback Host, which is the `loopback` transport: make such a proxy send `X-Forwarded-For` and `X-Forwarded-Proto` and name it in `GOBLIN_TRUST_PROXY`) with `Deprecation` and `Link` headers; from 1.6 Basic is off on upgraded installs too, unless `GOBLIN_LEGACY_BASIC=1`. Sessions are never throttle-checked, but only a correct password makes an address trusted during a many-address attack (a session cookie never does, so a stolen one is no way around the global lock, which also holds password changes). Only the `loopback` transport is exempt from the global sign-in lock: the Uptime Goblin computer itself, and what a proxy there that sends no forwarding headers relays with a loopback Host. Which lockout a failed sign-in counts against, the ssh tunnel's included, is in **Sign-in lockout key** below. The log names a trusted proxy that sends no `X-Forwarded-For` and a proxy on the Uptime Goblin computer that sends no forwarding headers at all, and a restart clears a lockout. A sign-in refused because other clients filled the queue no longer counts against you. A session cookie that another site's page makes the browser send (same-site) is refused unread, so it cannot keep an idle session alive. Signing out over HTTPS also ends the browser's plain-HTTP session. Every sign-in on the page and every password change is a full password check; the one-day grace for a replaced password and the cached check are kept for scripts using Basic only, so a browser or phone that still fills in the old password counts as failing.
- A session can end only sessions that signed in no later than it did. A session cookie taken from a lost device (or captured over plain HTTP) could otherwise press **Sign out other devices** in a loop and end every new sign-in before you could sign it out or change the password; now signing in again always outranks it, and its **Sign out other devices** ends the stolen session. The Account page marks a device that signed in after this one "Signed in later", without **Sign out**, and says what to do; `DELETE /api/account/sessions` answers `{revoked, kept}`, and signing out one such session answers 403 `session_newer`.
- **API for scripts.** The password change is `POST /api/account/password`; `POST /api/auth/password` still works as its deprecated alias and answers with a `Deprecation` header (a script that signs in with legacy Basic also gets the `Link` header every Basic answer carries). New: `POST /api/auth/login` and `POST /api/auth/logout`, `GET /api/session`, `GET /api/account/sessions`, and `DELETE /api/account/sessions` and `/api/account/sessions/<id>`. Their writes are never replayed for an `Idempotency-Key`; the sign-in and password-change bodies are limited to 4,096 bytes (a larger one gets 413), and the sign-outs take no body (none or `{}`). A 401 is JSON `{error, code, transport}`, and carries `WWW-Authenticate: Basic` only for scripts, and only while legacy Basic is on (off by default on new installs). Also new: `GET /api/notifications` and `GET /api/notifications/deliveries`, `POST /api/notifications/deliveries/<uid>/retry`, `POST /api/notifications/channels/<id>/resume`, `GET /api/notifications/channels/<id>/settings`, `PATCH /api/notifications/channels/<id>/settings` and `POST /api/notifications/channels/<id>/test` (ntfy and Gotify), `GET /api/notifications/webhook/public-key` and `POST /api/notifications/webhook/signing-secret` (replaces the signing key), and `GET /api/audit`, `GET /api/audit/export` and `GET /api/audit/verify`. Writes can answer 503 `storage_full` (a full disk) or `audit_unavailable` (the audit log cannot be written), with `Retry-After`. See [the HTTP API](docs/architecture.md#http-api).
- **`X-Request-Id`** on every answer, `/health` and refusals included: the id a proxy named in `GOBLIN_TRUST_PROXY` sent, when it is 1 to 64 letters, digits, dots, hyphens or underscores, otherwise a new UUID, so a client cannot put text of its own into Uptime Goblin's log. Uptime Goblin's log lines for unexpected failures end with `(request <id>)`, and each audit entry that a request made keeps it (**Request**, `request_id` in exports). See [headers](docs/architecture.md#headers-retries-and-busy-answers).
- Destination links: beyond the maintenance candidate's check, 1.5 also refuses a credential-like parameter after a route (`#/home&access_token=…`) or in a text directive (`#intro:~:text=…&access_token=…`), and startup removes it there from older saved links too, without logging it. A route's own query (`#/callback?x=1&…`) stays the application's.
- A cookie of Uptime Goblin's name that another site sets (another port of the same host, a site on a parent domain, or anyone who can inject a response over plain HTTP) never keeps a session alive. **Sign out** with such a cookie present ends this browser's session, removes Uptime Goblin's cookie and answers 200 with code `multiple_credentials`, and the sign-in page says to remove this site's cookies; a sign-out Uptime Goblin refuses for that reason keeps the page and says so. Signing in still ends every session the browser presented, and signing in or out over HTTPS every plain-HTTP session it still holds, beside any number of such cookies.
- **Sign out** starts the page again only once Uptime Goblin confirmed it. Without an answer the page stays and says the browser may still be signed in; a sign-out Uptime Goblin could not record on a full or failing disk is answered as such (503 `storage_full`, or 500), not as done. Such sessions are refused at once and removed by a retry, by **Sign out other devices**, hourly, or when Uptime Goblin stops; if they cannot be removed by then, the log says a restart accepts them again. The browser keeps its cookie until Uptime Goblin has recorded its sign-out, so pressing **Sign out** again from it removes the session (signing in again does too).
- After cancelling a sign-in, signing in again from any page clears the "Signed out" notice and reloads the readings; a configuration page that never loaded keeps its **Sign in**. Focus returns to the form after a save or a password change (also after signing in again), and to the page's heading when the control it came from has gone.
- One **Cancel** closes the **Sign in again** dialog for the Account page's **Sign out** buttons and the email form's **Save** and **Send test email** too, and the focus returns to the button. Signing in again shows the Account and System pages and Help as they are now, and the Account page lists the new session as this device. A sign-out failure that arrives after the menu was closed shows at the bottom of the page and stays in the menu until it is opened; after a failed sign-out the keyboard focus stays on **Sign out**.
- Phones and narrow windows: the top bar fits from 320 px wide (the user menu becomes a person icon up to 440 px, and the HOMELAB label goes up to 760 px), and the Account page's sessions table scrolls inside its box, so the page and the sign-in dialog keep the window's width. Each session's **Sign out** stays in view at the right of that box wherever the table is scrolled.

So does the signed-out notice's **Sign in** after **Cancel**, and the notice is not drawn (and read out) again when it already says so. **Account** in the user menu gives the focus to your name when no page opens (the Account page shows already, or you kept your unsaved changes at the prompt).

Remove this site’s cookies, then sign in." and stops refreshing. When you come back to it after removing the cookies it offers **Sign in**; if the other site removed its cookie, it carries on.

- A second Uptime Goblin on the same data directory exits 75 (`goblin.lock`). A stop requested while Uptime Goblin starts stops the start and exits 0. Startup snapshots leave `history.sqlite` out, and are on disk before the migration begins, so a power loss just after the first upgraded start can no longer leave the migrated data without its pinned way back to 1.4.x. A crash loop reuses its snapshot only while `config.json` and `auth.json` are unchanged, and every snapshot of 1.4.x data is pinned, so after restoring one and changing things under 1.4.x, the next upgrade keeps that newer state too. (After a code-only rollback nothing is migrated at the next upgrade, so no snapshot is taken: changes made under 1.4.x in between are in no snapshot.) A snapshot restore interrupted by a crash makes the next start of 1.5 refuse (exit status 78) with the command that finishes it, instead of starting on a half-restored directory; running it again keeps what the data directory held before the first attempt as the undo, also when the crash came after everything was in place, and a file that was not there before stays absent in it. A restore over an interrupted one that it does not finish (one without history over one that had set `history.sqlite` aside, say) keeps that one's record even when it is itself interrupted and run again, so 1.5 still refuses to start until that one is run again too; A restore lets Uptime Goblin start again only once the restored files and its undo are on disk, so a power loss just after it cannot leave an empty history behind. 1.4.x does not check for an interrupted restore: after a code rollback, keep it stopped until the restore has finished (the documented commands disable the service). Should it run in between, the re-run deletes nothing it wrote: files changed after the interrupted attempt (a password, settings, readings) are kept in a snapshot of their own, which it names. A restore replaces `config.json` and `auth.json` in one step, so they are never missing part way and 1.4.x cannot start without a password on a half-restored directory. The hidden copies a crashed restore leaves in the data directory (`.history.sqlite.restoring` and the like, as large as the files they copy) are listed by `goblin-admin status` and `goblin-admin snapshots` and removed by the next start of 1.5 or later or the next restore (the log and `restore-snapshot` say which; 1.4.x leaves them); those an interrupted restore still needs stay until it is run again. The next start of 1.5 or later, or the next restore, also removes an empty `snapshots/.restoring-…` folder left by a restore stopped before it moved anything (`goblin-admin snapshots` lists it as "Empty"). A restore over a damaged database (`file is not a database`) completes, moving the damaged file aside as it is, with its `-wal` and `-shm`. `goblin-admin` commands that change the data directory also refuse (exit status 75) while `run/status.json` names a running Uptime Goblin, since 1.4.x takes no `goblin.lock`; under Docker, where the Uptime Goblin container's processes cannot be seen, while it names a 1.4.x release at all. Under Docker a refusal because Uptime Goblin holds `goblin.lock` says `docker compose stop uptime-goblin`, not `sudo systemctl stop uptime-goblin`. `goblin-admin status` tells whether Uptime Goblin runs as those refusals do: a 1.5 Uptime Goblin by `goblin.lock`, a 1.4.x one by its process, and in a container of its own (`docker compose run`, or the kept 1.5 image after a Compose rollback) it says a 1.4.x Uptime Goblin "may be running". A 1.4.2, which keeps no `run/status.json` and takes no `goblin.lock`, is found natively by its process (a `server.mjs` whose data directory is this one): `status` says "running, version 1.4.2, … found by its process" and commands that change the data directory refuse (75). Where it cannot look (in a container, without `/proc`, another user's process) `status` says Uptime Goblin "may be running" and why, instead of "not running". Refusals that send you to `goblin-admin` print a command line that runs as printed. Those `goblin-admin` prints itself natively name the copy that runs and its data directory: the 1.5 copy kept after a code rollback names `/opt/uptime-goblin.previous/…` (`/opt/uptime-goblin` then holds 1.4.x, which has no `goblin-admin`), and an interrupted restore's line in `status` and `migrate --apply`'s refusal say to keep the service disabled until the restore has finished, then enable and start it.

Until the installer adds a `goblin-admin` command, run it as `sudo -u uptime-goblin node /opt/uptime-goblin/lib/cli/goblin-admin.mjs COMMAND --data-dir /var/lib/uptime-goblin`, with `env GOBLIN_SECRET_KEY_FILE=/etc/uptime-goblin/secret.key` before `node` for the key commands and `restore-snapshot`, since `sudo -u uptime-goblin` starts a clean environment (Compose: `docker compose run --rm --no-deps uptime-goblin node lib/cli/goblin-admin.mjs COMMAND`), with `COMMAND` replaced by the command and its options; run as root, a command that changes the data directory prints that form, naming the key file it was given, else `/etc/uptime-goblin/secret.key`, and the previous key file it was given (`key rewrap`: else a placeholder).
- The native installer refuses, changing nothing (exit status 2), a release older than the installed one unless `--allow-downgrade` is given, and warns but goes on when the installed version is not one it can compare. It runs one at a time (`flock` on `/run/uptime-goblin-install.lock`; a second run stops before anything changes). From just before it stops the service until the new release is verified or the previous one restored, a hangup no longer ends it (its output goes to `/run/uptime-goblin-install.log`), and Ctrl-C or `SIGTERM` restores the previous version at once instead of leaving an unverified release or a missing `/opt/uptime-goblin`. It checks in the background after 30 seconds, then less often, up to every 10 minutes. A page whose build Uptime Goblin no longer has does the same.
- Request bodies are refused only for shapes that can reach a prototype: `__proto__`, or `prototype` inside `constructor`. A source upgraded from 1.4.x with a metric label named `constructor` or `prototype` can be saved and tested again.
- **Requests Uptime Goblin serves (security).** Only a request target that is a path: `//host/…`, `/\host/…` and absolute `http://host/…` targets were served by their path, unseen by a reverse-proxy rule written for that path (such as a sign-in rate limit), and now get 400, as does a request with no `Host` or several `Host` lines. A body larger than its route takes gets 413 "Request is too large" (it was 400) and the connection is closed. One whose `Content-Length` says so gets it after the `Host` and request-target checks: until a credential signs in, every request is held to the default limit (150,000 bytes; a public route, the sign-in, `/health` and the web app's files, to its own), so a request without a credential cannot tell from a 413 which paths and methods exist or what they take (1.5 builds so far held it to the matched route's limit, a read's none included, before the credential); its route's own limit applies once it signed in. One sent without a length (chunked) gets it at once on a read once signed in, and otherwise when a handler reads past the limit, so a request that fails the credential or the route's own checks first gets that answer instead, and is closed unread. Uptime Goblin no longer reads and discards an unread body of any size, after a refusal included, while a body within the route's limit that Uptime Goblin answered without reading is still read so the connection stays usable. `Expect: 100-continue` gets `100 Continue` only when Uptime Goblin reads the body, after the Host, the credential and the route's own checks. A request whose headers have not all arrived within 20 seconds, or the whole request within 30, gets 408 and is closed; Uptime Goblin checks every second. Before, Node's defaults applied: 60 seconds for the headers and 5 minutes for the whole request, checked every 30 seconds, so a slow client kept its connection for up to 90 seconds, or 5½ minutes, before its 408. At most 256 connections are open at once, and at most 32 from one client (one address, an IPv6 one by its /64), so one client cannot take them all and lock everyone else out: one more is closed at once, without an answer. `GOBLIN_MAX_CONNECTIONS_PER_CLIENT` (new) changes the share, from 8 to 256 (any other value refuses the start); a proxy named in `GOBLIN_TRUST_PROXY` carries many clients and has none. Compose passes the setting from `.env`. One allowance per address is a deliberate tradeoff: wherever many users reach Uptime Goblin from one address they share one allowance of 32. That is every browser on the published port under Docker Compose when Docker hands their connections over from its gateway, the clients of a proxy that `GOBLIN_TRUST_PROXY` does not name, everything on the Uptime Goblin computer that connects from 127.0.0.1 (the ssh tunnel and a proxy there included, unless `GOBLIN_TRUST_PROXY=loopback`), and the devices of one IPv6 /64. Name the proxy in `GOBLIN_TRUST_PROXY`, or raise `GOBLIN_MAX_CONNECTIONS_PER_CLIENT`. A connection the share closes is logged, at most one line a minute for all clients together, naming the last client and how many were closed since the previous line ("Uptime Goblin closed … without an answer …"; those closed within the minute after a line are written once the minute has passed), and never anything the connection sent. A start records the share, and a change to it, with the other security settings in the audit log; the first start after an update from an earlier 1.5 build reads the share that build did not record as the default, 32.
- **Sign-in lockout key (security).** Which lockout a failed sign-in counts against, which is also the address its session and audit entries show, no longer depends on a header the client chooses, unless a proxy that `GOBLIN_TRUST_PROXY` names passes it on: everything that connects to Uptime Goblin from a loopback address (127.0.0.0/8 or `::1`) counts against the ssh tunnel's lockout (`loopback`) unless `GOBLIN_TRUST_PROXY` names it, whatever its Host or forwarding headers say; a proxy `GOBLIN_TRUST_PROXY` names is counted by the last `X-Forwarded-For` entry (else its own address), and anyone else by their own address (a proxy on the Uptime Goblin host that connects to Uptime Goblin's own network address included). 1.5 builds so far also looked at the page address (`Origin`), the Host and the forwarding headers: through a proxy on the Uptime Goblin host that connects to Uptime Goblin at a loopback address and that `GOBLIN_TRUST_PROXY` does not name, a remote client could choose between the ssh tunnel's lockout and the proxy's address, and so get twice the guesses. It now has the tunnel's lockout only, which it can still lock out. Only the `loopback` transport is exempt from the global lock of a many-address attack: it holds every other request with the `loopback` key, whatever password any of them proved, through the tunnel or through the proxy. Through a proxy on the Uptime Goblin host that sends no forwarding headers, trusted or not, a request that keeps a loopback Host still is that transport, as the docs say, and with `GOBLIN_TRUST_PROXY=loopback` such a proxy passes on whatever forwarding headers the client wrote, which then choose its key: make every proxy on the host send forwarding headers and name it in `GOBLIN_TRUST_PROXY`. While plain-HTTP sign-in is allowed, the log also names, once an hour, a proxy on the Uptime Goblin host that sends forwarding headers but that `GOBLIN_TRUST_PROXY` does not name ("A reverse proxy on this computer that GOBLIN_TRUST_PROXY does not name sends Uptime Goblin requests with forwarding headers…"): sign-ins and Basic through it are accepted as plain HTTP from wherever it took them. IPv6 clients are still counted by their /64, so on a home network every IPv6 device shares one lockout; reach Uptime Goblin over IPv4 to keep them apart.
- **`GOBLIN_TRUST_PROXY` (security).** The IPv6 prefixes whose addresses carry an IPv4 address in their last 32 bits, the same /96 forms sources and the webhook read (see below), refuse the start like `0.0.0.0/0`, and so does any range that holds one: the NAT64 prefix `64:ff9b::/96` (and the local-use `64:ff9b:1::/96`), the IPv4-translated `::ffff:0:0:0/96` and the IPv4-compatible `::/96`. A NAT64 gateway or a translator connects from them for IPv4 clients, so each stands for the whole IPv4 internet. A range inside one counts as the IPv4 range it stands for, as one inside the IPv4-mapped `::ffff:0:0/96` already did; `::` and `::1` alone stay IPv6. 6to4 (`2002::/16`), which sources and the webhook also read, is not one of them here: a 6to4 range is an IPv6 network, held to the IPv6 widths (`2002::/16` itself is wider than /32 and refused). The start warning for a wide range now covers anything wider than /24 (IPv4) or /64 (IPv6); 1.5 builds so far warned only above /16 or /48.
- **Request bodies (security).** UTF-8 only: a `charset` other than UTF-8 gets 415, and bytes that are not UTF-8 get 400 (they were saved as U+FFFD), as does a `\u` escape of half a surrogate pair, which became U+FFFD once written out. Text that `config.json` holds with such a half (1.4.x accepted it, in a dashboard name or in a rule key copied from a measurement key) is made whole (U+FFFD) once at start, logged by its place in the file, never by text: the web app sends those values back with every Appearance or source save, which was refused. Two alert rules of one source whose keys differed only in such a half (which 1.4.3 loaded) are then one key, which refused the start: the first rule is kept and the later ones are removed, logged by place. A Prometheus label filter or a JSON mapping path made whole this way still matches the exporter's text holding that half: when nothing matches a filter or a path step holding U+FFFD as the exporter sent it, the exporter's text is compared made whole (a match as sent comes first). Which half the filter or path held is lost, so the mapping keeps its reading and its alert rules while the exporter sends neither U+FFFD itself nor another half at that place: one that sends U+FFFD there has that series or member read instead, and one that sends two different halves there (texts that read the same made whole) has the mapping read nothing, never their sum or the first of them, with a note on the source. The start log says this once, after naming the places. A member name given twice in one object, at any depth, gets 400 (a proxy or log reading the first one saw another request than Uptime Goblin's), and so does nesting deeper than 64 levels. Writes that take no body (refresh, sign-out, removing a source, signing out sessions) accept only none or `{}`; sign-in and the password change refuse fields they do not have (`invalid_request`). Settings and source changes are checked before they take their turn, so a malformed one writes no audit entry, takes no place in the queue and gets its 400 also while the disk is full. `If-Match` names one revision (`12`, `"12"`, `W/"12"`); `*`, lists and spellings such as `0x1` or `12.0` get 400 `if_match_invalid`. A saved source keeps its type (400; the editor already locked it), also in a connection test, which handed its saved credentials to the other type's collector. The connection test's `serviceId` is a saved source's ID or `null`, and a UniFi site a site UUID or a local site name (one saved earlier keeps loading). `range`, `doc` and `instance` may be given once. Names, groups, usernames, metric labels, units and the dashboard name and subtitle refuse control characters, line separators and text-direction overrides, which could make a source name in an alert read as another; values saved earlier are repaired once at start, logged by field, never by text. A Prometheus mapping's label filters (its `labels` values) are not display text: they must equal the exporter's label values, so they keep any character but NUL, as before, and this repair leaves them as they are (only half of a surrogate pair is made whole in them, as above). Each text field of the source editor and the settings page (Appearance, Email) takes at most as many characters as Uptime Goblin accepts, so a longer value can no longer be typed only to be refused on Save: **Accepted HTTP statuses** 200 (it was 256), display name, group and UniFi site 100, addresses 2,048, endpoint path 500, username 200, password and API key 8,192, rule measurement 150, time zone 100, SMTP host 253, email addresses 254 and app password 1,024.

A key file may still be a symbolic link to a regular file (a systemd credential or a Compose secret often is); a link to a FIFO is refused the same way. The plain-HTTP step, the proxy preflight and the checks of `GOBLIN_LEGACY_BASIC`, `GOBLIN_ALLOW_UNLIMITED`, `GOBLIN_SUPERVISED`, `GOBLIN_MAX_CONNECTIONS_PER_CLIENT` and `GOBLIN_TRUST_PROXY` read those settings, and `GOBLIN_INSECURE_HTTP_SESSIONS`, as systemd gives them to Uptime Goblin, as the password step does (they read `/etc/uptime-goblin.env` alone, so a drop-in that named a trusted proxy and decided plain HTTP was refused, and one that set a value 1.5 refuses passed, and the upgrade was rolled back): one the new release refuses ends the install with "This release of Uptime Goblin would refuse to start with the settings systemd would give it", and one decided in a way the installer cannot read (a specifier or `PassEnvironment=` in a drop-in, a failing `systemctl show-environment`) with "… as systemd would give it to Uptime Goblin could not be checked", changing nothing (an `EnvironmentFile=` named with a specifier or a file pattern ends the install earlier, at the `HOST` and `PORT` check, unless a file read after it sets both). The warning about a public host without a trusted proxy, and whether `--allow-insecure-http` adds its line, take those settings as systemd gives them too (a proxy named in a drop-in drew "GOBLIN_TRUST_PROXY is not set", and the option's `=1` overrode a drop-in's `GOBLIN_INSECURE_HTTP_SESSIONS=0`).
- **Docker image supply chain (security).** The `Dockerfile` is the maintenance line's, byte for byte: the base image is pinned by digest (`node:24-bookworm-slim@sha256:…`; Dependabot proposes a new one), the build installs Debian's `libpcre2-8-0` security update (CVE-2026-103111), which the pinned image predates, at exactly `10.42-1+deb12u2` (the build therefore needs Debian's package servers as well as npm's registry, and fails when Debian no longer offers that version), and npm, npx, corepack and Yarn are removed from the image after the dependencies are installed: Uptime Goblin runs `node` alone. A test of the running image checks that its code is root's and not writable by others, that `/app/data` and its files are private to the runtime user, that no package manager is left, and that its `libpcre2-8-0` is that version or later.
- **Container and service limits (security).** `compose.yaml` limits the container to 1 GiB of memory (no swap on top, on a kernel that accounts swap), 1 CPU and 256 processes, written in it rather than read from `.env` (Docker reads `0`, and a negative process limit, as no limit, and Compose cannot check what `.env` gives): an override file changes them. The systemd unit sets `MemoryMax=1G`, `MemorySwapMax=0`, `CPUQuota=200%` and `TasksMax=256` (a drop-in changes them). `compose.yaml` also names the user itself (`user: '1000:1000'`, the image's `node` account), so an image re-tagged as `uptime-goblin:local` cannot run Uptime Goblin as root, and allows 4096 open files and no core dumps, which would write the encryption key and passwords from Uptime Goblin's memory to disk. The systemd unit sets `LimitNOFILE=4096` and `LimitCORE=0` too, and adds `ProtectClock=yes`, `ProtectHostname=yes`, `ProtectKernelLogs=yes`, `RemoveIPC=yes` and `SystemCallFilter=@system-service @pkey` (any other system call fails with `EPERM`), the set Debian's `systemd-logind` runs with; measured with Uptime Goblin under real systemd, which rates the unit 1.7 instead of 3.3. A supervised Uptime Goblin reads its cgroup's memory, process and CPU limits back at every start and refuses to start without one (see "A supervised Uptime Goblin refuses to start without its memory, CPU and process limits" below), and warns when swap of its memory is allowed after all ("Uptime Goblin's memory can be written to disk: …"), or, on a host with swap, when it cannot read its swap limit ("Uptime Goblin cannot tell whether its memory can be swapped out: …"; a kernel without swap accounting has none, and Docker and systemd start Uptime Goblin without the one they were given): an override file can set a limit to `0`, a drop-in can set one to `infinity` (or `MemorySwapMax=`), and an LXC host may not delegate a controller. It can read them under cgroup v2 only: on a Linux host with cgroup v1 or a hybrid layout it cannot verify them, and refuses to start unless `GOBLIN_ALLOW_UNLIMITED` names them. Core dumps allowed after all (`LimitCORE=` in a drop-in, `docker run` without `--ulimit core=0`) are named whatever the cgroup layout. The documented `docker run` line for `restore-snapshot` carries the same user and limits.
- **Source answers (security).** Collectors and Website checks connect only to the addresses the destination check approved, never to a second DNS answer for the same name: a short-lived or hostile DNS answer could pass the check and then send a source's request, with its API key, to a service listening only on the Uptime Goblin host or to `169.254.169.254`. Connection setup now counts towards the request's own time limit (fetch's separate limit of about 10 seconds is gone). A body or list entry of an unexpected shape is reported as "Source returned an unexpected response. Check the API URL and that the source version is supported." (it read "Check the address and credential format"), and other JavaScript errors are shown as fixed text. Names, IDs and units from a source become one line of text: each run of control characters and line or paragraph separators (U+2028, U+2029) becomes one space, and the direction embeddings, overrides and isolates (U+202A–U+202E, U+2066–U+2069) are removed, as Uptime Goblin refuses them in names you save. Labels and units also lose the direction marks (U+061C, U+200E, U+200F) and white space at either end; measurement keys keep them, as 1.4.3 did. The source's **Metric thresholds** editor now keeps such a key exactly when you choose it from the **Measurement** list or edit its rule: it trimmed the key, as 1.4.3 did, so a rule on a key made from an ID or path ending in a space was saved under another key and never fired. A key you type is still trimmed. A long name or ID is shortened instead of failing the whole source, and a key made from it keeps at most 150 characters (a prefix and a hash), so an alert rule can use it. A reading therefore has another key than 1.4.3 gave it, or is left out, in the cases [troubleshooting](docs/troubleshooting.md#measurement-keys-after-an-upgrade-from-14x) lists, among them an ID or path holding a control character, U+2028, U+2029 or a direction embedding, override or isolate (not an AMP metric name, whose key turns each character but an ASCII letter or digit into `_`, as 1.4.3 did); a UniFi device, Proxmox node, guest or storage, Prowlarr indexer or AMP key over 150 characters (paths were shortened already) or a Proxmox key starting with `__`, on which 1.4.3 could save no alert rule; a long disk path whose key 1.4.3 cut inside a character such as an emoji; and some IDs that are blank, are not text or are a number (0 among them), and a Proxmox guest without an ID. Such a reading starts a new history, and an alert rule on its old key matches nothing until you choose the reading again in the source's **Metric thresholds**. Half a surrogate pair in an ID other than an AMP metric name becomes U+FFFD, as it does in a saved alert rule, so a rule on such a key, which never fired, now matches. An entry without a usable name or ID is left out with a note, and so are readings whose key an earlier reading of the same source already has: an ID or path listed twice (1.4.3 dropped such readings without a word), or two different ones that now make one key, such as `/x y` and the same path with a control character in place of the space ([troubleshooting](docs/troubleshooting.md)). Built-in readings outside their unit's range (negative counts, sizes, rates or durations, percentages above 100) are left out; custom mappings keep their values. UniFi Integration API lists stop at 100 pages and 20,000 entries each (the card's note names both limits), devices and clients are read for the first 50 sites (with a note), a site listed twice is read once, and one collection makes at most 1,000 list requests; a page longer than asked for no longer fails with a JavaScript error. Prometheus samples and their labels count towards the collection's 750,000-value budget: one value per sample kept and one per label read, the labels of a line then left out (at a 65th label, a repeated label name or a bad escape) included. A label-heavy endpoint therefore fails as too complex, for example from 93,751 samples of seven labels or 11,539 of 64: narrow what the exporter reports. When every sample line is kept and has six labels or fewer, the 100,000-sample limit applies first. A Prometheus body may also have at most 1,000,000 lines, comment and blank lines included (ten for each of the 100,000 samples): one with more, which 1.4.3 read, fails with "Prometheus source exceeds the 1,000,000 line limit". Response headers over 16 KiB are reported as such and not retried. Nginx Proxy Manager, CrowdSec and AMP sign-ins must answer with a usable session token, or nothing is kept and the next collection signs in again. Email text also turns C1 controls and the line and paragraph separators into a space and drops the direction embeddings, overrides and isolates, as names refuse them (the direction marks stay, as names you save keep them), and Uptime Goblin limits an SMTP server's reply to 64 KiB itself. The history CSV export shares the audit export's formula guard (`public/shared/csv.js`).
- **Requests whose session ends part way (security).** **Replace signing key**, **Retry** and **Resume** check the session again right before they act: one that was signed out, revoked or had its password changed while Uptime Goblin read what they act on gets 401, with nothing replaced, requeued or released and nothing recorded as done (once they have acted, the 401 still keeps their outcome for the retry after signing in again, as before). **Sign out other devices**, and signing out one device, from a session that ends while they read answer 401: the sessions they had already signed out stay signed out (recorded as Uptime Goblin removing them, "Removed 1 sessions signed out by a session that ended before it could save that", naming the session whenever it removed only one, even one of several it was deleting, and never the session that ended), but nothing else is ended for them and nothing is recorded as theirs. One whose session had ended before Uptime Goblin acted on it (signed out while Uptime Goblin still waited for the request's body) ends nothing at all. **Retry** is neither offered nor accepted for a failed notification whose source was deleted or whose kind of alert was switched off since (409 `delivery_not_retryable`): Uptime Goblin said it was sending it again and then skipped it. An email digest is retried as a whole, so this holds for every notification of an email holding such a one, with a refusal that names the other notification; so does the refusal of a notification whose email holds another that Retry refuses for its own reason (its alert's later notification was delivered: 409 `delivery_outdated`; it is being sent, waits for its first attempt or was cancelled: 409 `delivery_not_retryable`). At most two audit exports are built and sent at once (another gets 503 `busy` with `Retry-After: 5`): each keeps its place until it has been built and recorded and its answer has gone, and an answer its client stops taking for 30 seconds is closed, so clients that read only the headers can no longer hold any number of 16 MiB bodies, and clients that leave while their export is built can no longer start any number of builds (a build goes on after its client left, and keeps its place until it is done). NDJSON exports write U+2028, U+2029, C1 controls and text-direction characters as `\u` escapes (a reader that splits lines the Unicode way cut a row in two there), and CSV cells, the history export's included, leave the direction embeddings, overrides and isolates out; the direction marks (U+061C, U+200E, U+200F) stay, as names you save keep them, and a cell that starts like a formula after them is guarded too.
- **Database statements (security).** Text that no database stores as given is refused when a statement binds it (a 500-class error, the row alone for a batch of readings), never stored altered: a NUL, which let a value of any size past SQLite's length checks, and half of a surrogate pair, which SQLite stored as U+FFFD, so two different keys became one row. The history store's alert rules, which bind through statements of their own, compare and store a rule key made whole (U+FFFD) instead, so a rule whose key holds half a pair (a hand edit of `config.json`) now fires on its reading; the API refuses such a key outright (see request bodies). Where such text comes from outside (a source's error, a mail server's message ID, a cut between the halves of an emoji), Uptime Goblin stores it with U+FFFD and without NUL instead, as SQLite did; device IDs and names a source sends are made whole the same way, so an alert rule on such a measurement now fires. History keeps 300 characters of a failed collection's reason, cut between characters, and stores an error object as its text instead of failing the whole reading. A database path is opened as a file path, never as a SQLite URI, snapshot copies included, and a relative `GOBLIN_DATA_DIR` is made absolute at the start: a `GOBLIN_DATA_DIR` such as `file:data` opened another file than the one Uptime Goblin locked and protected (left readable by others), and wrote a snapshot's copy of `control.sqlite` elsewhere, failing the pre-upgrade snapshot. Uptime Goblin's connections refuse what SQLite does not mark harmless (such as the `pragma_*` tables) in triggers and views (`trusted_schema` off), and `goblin-admin check-data` reports any trigger or view in `control.sqlite` or `history.sqlite`, and any other entry of their catalog that is not a table or an index as SQLite writes one, and exits 1 (Uptime Goblin creates none; a trigger runs inside Uptime Goblin's own writes, also one whose catalog entry spells its type `TRIGGER`, which SQLite runs as any other while a lookup of `trigger` missed it); a start refuses either database holding one (exit status 78, `schema_planted`) having written nothing but `goblin.lock`, which it creates where it is missing (on a disk already full it has also deleted its space reserve, `run/space-reserve`), and a snapshot holding one is listed as damaged and not restored. Statements are built only through the `sql` template tag (an array made at run time and dressed as a template, the query class reached through `.constructor`, a directive without a dialect or a column of another table is refused at run time; an exact imitation of a template object cannot be told from one there, which is why the lint refuses every use of the tag but as a tag), migrations may create only the tables and indexes the schema describes, the history store keeps its connection to itself, and the dialect a database renders statements with cannot be replaced (a repository that set `db.dialect` had any text prepared for a `sql` query). `npm run lint:sql` finds SQL in any case and split over `+`, refuses every use of the tag but as a tag, and keeps `fragment()` and `directive()` (which write any string as SQL text) to the dialects, however the module's path is spelt (`../db/./sql.mjs` and `../db/SQL.mjs` passed), however the import is written (a comment inside its braces, or a name written as a string, `{ 'fragment' as f }`, passed), and with `import()` or a loader naming one literal path with nothing added to it (`import('../db/sq' + 'l.mjs')` passed). Both lints refuse a module named other than by a plain relative path to a `.mjs` or `.js` file, a `node:` builtin or a package name: Node reads a `%`-escape, `\`, a space, a query or a fragment in a path as another file, and a URL or an absolute path as any module (a `data:` URL re-exporting `node:sqlite` passed). Both refuse a symbolic link among the server modules (Node loads one under its target's name). They read the source with a scanner that now reads a regular expression after the `)` of `if`, `while`, `for` or `with` (one holding a backquote hid all the code after it from both lints), a division after `++`, `--`, a property named like a keyword or a name in any script (`é / …`), a `//` comment as ending at `\r`, U+2028 or U+2029, a `\r\n` line continuation as part of its string and a first `#!` line as a comment; it refuses what it cannot tell (a `/` after `}` or after `of`) and a `\` in code (an escaped name: `pr\u0065pare` is `prepare`). They also check the modules the server code reaches by a relative import (`public/shared/*.js`, or a hidden `lib/.x.mjs`). `npm run lint:sql` freezes every statement of `lib/store.mjs`, lower-case ones without values and every literal handed to its connection included, and refuses a value joined to one with `+` (`'DELETE … WHERE service=?' + clause` passed: the freeze hashed the literal alone); the values its frozen statements splice in with `${…}` are checked by review, not by the lint. The two modules that hold a raw connection hand it one literal, or a variable at a site the lint lists, bound there as listed and nowhere else in that function (`const text = …` before `exec(text)` passed); they make each connection as `name = new DatabaseSync(…);`, import `node:sqlite` by name and nothing at run time, and use a connection only through those calls and `close`, `isOpen` and `isTransaction`, never destructured, aliased, passed on or returned but where the lint lists it (`const { prepare } = this.#db` passed) and never with `DatabaseSync`'s other methods, which run SQL text or replace the database (`createTagStore()` and `deserialize()` passed). Elsewhere the lint refuses `prepare`, a connection's `exec` (also as `?.()`, taken as a value or destructured), `createTagStore`, `deserialize`, `applyChangeset`, `loadExtension` and the like, a connection's members reached by a computed key or `Reflect`, and those words, `exposeStoreConnections` and `getBuiltinModule` written as a string however it is spelt (an escape, a template, `'pre' + 'pare'`). A key computed at run time from other values is beyond the lint; the history store's connection is closed to it all the same (`store.db` throws unless the test switch is on). That switch is a module of its own, which `npm run lint:imports` lets only `lib/store.mjs` import. `npm run lint:imports` reads import and export declarations as declarations (a name written as a string or a comment inside one passed, and one of another shape is refused), counts `require()`, `process.getBuiltinModule()` and `createRequire()` called by name with one literal and nothing added to it (also as `?.()`) as imports (`getBuiltinModule('node:' + 'sqlite')` passed), and refuses as an import it cannot follow any other use of those names (`.call()`, a reference taken out of `process` or imported under another name passed), those names written as a string, `process` taken as a value or a member of it other than the ordinary ones (`process.binding('contextify')` passed), `globalThis` or `global` taken as a value or with a computed member or their `process`, `eval`, `Function`, `global` or `globalThis` (`globalThis.eval(…)` passed), `eval`, `Function`, and a function's constructor reached by its name (`.constructor`, `['constructor']` and `{ constructor: F } = f` passed; the string `'constructor'` stays allowed compared or looked up in a list of literals); it refuses `node:module`, `node:vm`, `node:inspector` and `node:repl` (each loads or runs code from a string), a relative path that names no file exactly as spelt, inside the tree and through no symbolic link (`../Store-Connections.mjs` loaded the test switch on macOS). A module name, loader or constructor computed at run time some other way (picked from a list of an object's properties, say), and a worker or a child process running code of its own, are beyond it.
- **Durable alert delivery.** Every notification is saved before it is sent, in the same write that records the alert's change, and copied into a delivery queue in `control.sqlite`: a restart, an upgrade or a crash no longer loses one, and a recovery never arrives before its alert on the same channel. Failed sends are retried (30 seconds rising to 15 minutes, for up to 24 hours; a `429` or `503` with `Retry-After` holds the whole channel up to an hour). Email keeps its one-message digests, now with a stable `Message-ID` per message on every attempt; a message is only ever sent again whole, so one `Message-ID` never carries two different messages, and messages waiting to go again never hold up the rest of the email queue. A long queue on one channel never delays the other's notifications. A notification is not sent if its source was deleted or its kind of alert was switched off while it waited. Saving email settings no longer drops alert email that was waiting (1.4.x skipped it): it is sent with the new settings, to the new recipient. Webhook notifications still waiting likewise go to a changed `GOBLIN_WEBHOOK_URL` after the restart, signed with the same key ([changing the receiver](docs/notifications.md#the-webhook)). After restoring a backup or a snapshot, or when `control.sqlite` was copied or moved (a volume copy, a new disk), notifications that were waiting before are not sent ("Not sent: Uptime Goblin's control database is a different file than before…"), nor are alert changes not yet queued; the log says how many.
- **Configure → Notifications** shows whether every alert got through: a summary, each channel's state in words (Working, Retrying, Paused, Refusing notifications, …) with its counts and last error, what needs attention, and the delivery history with filters and **Load more**; the history keeps finished notifications 7 days (at most 50,000) and failed ones 30 days (at most 5,000). **Retry** sends a failed notification again (asking first for one that may already have arrived), **Resume** a paused channel. Banners say when the disk is full, when notifications are not being saved, when a channel is paused or refusing, when alerts were not queued because the webhook's backlog of 10,000 was full (for 24 hours after the last; the webhook's card and the System page count them since the start, and the log says so at most once a minute), and when notifications failed. Only channels that are set up and switched on count: a webhook whose `GOBLIN_WEBHOOK_URL` was removed, or email that is switched off, is left out of the summary, **Needs attention** and the banners, and its notifications offer no **Retry** (409 `channel_not_set_up`), since Uptime Goblin would only skip them. The Email page, the Alerts page, the alerts window and the System page read the saved status, and say "Delivery status is unavailable" when Uptime Goblin cannot read it instead of showing older figures as current; the Email page counts the messages waiting to retry apart from newer ones waiting for their first message (`notifications.email.retrying`). A channel says Retrying or Waiting before the next try only while notifications are still to go or its wait has not ended, a notification skipped just before a send counts no attempt for it, and an email that was never accepted says "Grouped into one email with others" rather than "Sent as one email with others". The Notifications page works with the keyboard alone and at 320 px wide; **Try again** in a list that could not be read gives the focus to its first row once the read works.
- **Pause instead of retrying in vain.** A webhook answering `401`, `403`, `404`, `410` or a redirect, an untrusted certificate, an address that is not allowed, or an email sign-in failure pauses that channel: its notifications are held, the other channel says so while the alert switches allow it (the email explains that they are held, never that readings are not saved), and it resumes with **Resume**, an email settings save, an accepted test email, or a probe (every 15 minutes for causes that may pass, and at every start). A paused channel switched off while Uptime Goblin was stopped (email switched off, also under 1.4.x after a rollback; `GOBLIN_WEBHOOK_URL` removed) leaves its pause at the next start, and the other channel hears that it works again (while **Evaluate alerts** and **Notify when a source recovers** are on), as when email is switched off in a running Uptime Goblin: no probe could end that pause, so the other channel's receiver showed the pause for good. Three refused notifications in a row show "Refusing notifications".
- **Signed webhooks.** The body is 1.4.2's, unchanged. Requests add `X-Goblin-Delivery` (the same on every attempt of one notification) and `X-Goblin-Signature` (`t=…,v1a=<key id>:<signature>`: Ed25519 over the delivery id, the time and the body), and send `User-Agent: Uptime-Goblin` (1.4.x sent Node's default). Uptime Goblin generates the key pair and seals the private key with its new encryption key; no page, API answer, export, log line or audit entry ever holds the private key. Receivers verify with the public key, which is not a secret: **Configure → Notifications → Show public key**, or `GET /api/notifications/webhook/public-key` for scripts, with a Node.js and a Python example and a test vector in [verifying signatures](docs/notifications.md#verifying-signatures). **Replace signing key** makes a new pair and keeps the previous key signing for 24 hours (a second `v1a`, each with its own key id); potentially exposed keys from formerly revealable fields never overlap; Uptime Goblin keeps one previous key, so a second **Replace signing key** within those 24 hours stops the older one at once, and the page says so before it does. Pre-release builds signed with an HMAC secret that any admin could show (`POST /api/notifications/webhook/signing-secret/reveal`, now gone, like its 10-an-hour limit): the first start of this build replaces that secret with a new key pair, logs that it did, and never signs with HMAC again, so give receivers the public key; without `GOBLIN_WEBHOOK_URL` it removes the secret, logs that, and makes the key pair at the first start with a webhook. A key pair made in place of signing material Uptime Goblin had (such a secret, also one removed earlier; a stored value that is not an Ed25519 key; a key the encryption key no longer opens, after `goblin-admin key reset`) cannot sign beside it, so that start pauses the webhook: it holds every notification and sends nothing (not even unsigned while the key cannot be saved yet), no probe ends the pause, and **Resume**, once receivers have the public key, sends what waited, signed with the new key (before the key is saved, **Resume** is refused with 503 `signing_unavailable` and the hold stays; **Replace signing key** saves the hold with the new key, and holds the webhook in the same way when it has no key to keep signing, also before the start could read the settings); a receiver that still checked the old one would otherwise have refused each alert, and a `400` or `422` ended it as **Failed** ([a new signing key waits for Resume](docs/notifications.md#a-new-signing-key-waits-for-resume)). The first successfully preserved key pair of settings that never held one (a new install, an upgrade from 1.4.x) does not pause the webhook. Uptime Goblin never sends an unsigned webhook. If signing settings cannot be read or no usable signing key is available, delivery waits. With newer read-only settings, a usable current key may keep signing only when no saved signing hold applies; saved holds stay effective. **Replace signing key** always refuses such settings. **Resume** cannot clear an immutable saved hold or resume without a usable current key. These compatibility refusals answer 409 `signing_compatibility` with no `Retry-After`: "A newer Uptime Goblin saved these signing settings. Upgrade Uptime Goblin or restore a compatible backup before changing them.". A missing usable key in writable settings instead answers 503 `signing_unavailable` with `Retry-After: 60`; concurrent changes answer 409 `signing_conflict`. A stored key that is neither such a secret nor an Ed25519 private key is replaced in the same way at a start with a webhook, with a line of its own ("…could not be read as an Ed25519 key…"); an Ed25519 key stored in another PEM form is kept. The private keys live in settings fields the HMAC builds never open. Retired authority is treated as potentially disclosed and is never adopted, even in another PEM form; unreadable retired fields taint both active keys unless distinctness is provable. Replacement is fresh, atomic and held for Resume. URL-unset cleanup creates no key. Earlier HMAC builds can read the preserved schema but cannot change it, so those older builds show no signing secret and may send unsigned webhooks; this release never does. `X-Goblin-Signature` is Uptime Goblin's own format: Standard Webhooks libraries do not verify it. A request not answered within 8 seconds is sent once more after 2 minutes; an answer counts as soon as its status arrives, however long its body takes. Link-local, multicast and reserved addresses are refused, also inside NAT64 (`64:ff9b::/96`, `64:ff9b:1::/96`), IPv4-compatible, IPv4-translated and 6to4 IPv6 addresses; loopback and `http:` still work.
- **Addresses inside IPv6 addresses.** Sources, connection tests and the SMTP server, like the webhook, are now judged by the IPv4 address that a NAT64 (`64:ff9b::/96`, `64:ff9b:1::/96`), IPv4-compatible, IPv4-translated or 6to4 (`2002::/16`) address carries, also when a host name resolves to one: `64:ff9b::a9fe:a9fe` is refused like `169.254.169.254`, and one that carries `127.0.0.1` needs `GOBLIN_ALLOW_LOOPBACK=1`, like `127.0.0.1` itself. 1.4.x allowed such addresses.
- **Encryption key.** Every start opens Uptime Goblin's keyring, with a key from `GOBLIN_SECRET_KEY_FILE` (the native installer writes `/etc/uptime-goblin/secret.key` after checking it opens existing data), a systemd credential, a Compose secret (`compose.secret.yaml`), `GOBLIN_SECRET_KEY` (removed from Uptime Goblin's environment once read), or a `secret.key` Uptime Goblin generates in the data directory with a warning. The keyring is created only once a start has passed every other check, so a refused start leaves no keyring and no generated `secret.key` behind; a key refusal comes after the start took `goblin.lock`, which it creates where it is missing (on a disk already full it has also deleted its space reserve, `run/space-reserve`). The System page shows the key's source and fingerprint, the disk and the notifications. `goblin-admin key …` reads the key from its own environment, never from `/etc/uptime-goblin.env` (which only systemd reads), so the documented command lines and the refusals name the key file: `sudo -u uptime-goblin env GOBLIN_SECRET_KEY_FILE=/etc/uptime-goblin/secret.key node /opt/uptime-goblin/lib/cli/goblin-admin.mjs key status --data-dir /var/lib/uptime-goblin`. A key command that finds no key never calls it lost: `key status` says the key did not reach it and how to give it, and `key reset` refuses, writing no key and no keyring, rather than generate a `secret.key` in the data directory that the service never reads (Uptime Goblin would refuse to start, and the webhook signing key would be gone), unless given `--use-data-dir-key`. The installer refuses, changing nothing, a systemd credential `orbit-secret-key` set in `/etc/systemd/system/uptime-goblin.service` itself, which it replaces with its own unit: put it in a drop-in (`sudo systemctl edit uptime-goblin`). It reads the drop-ins in systemd's order (a `LoadCredential=` file outranks `SetCredential=`, which stands in only when that file is not there; an empty assignment clears its own list), checks the key a `SetCredential=` line holds against the data like a key file, before it stops Uptime Goblin, and never prints it (its messages name it `SetCredential=orbit-secret-key:…`). A `SetCredential=` key is plain text in a drop-in every local account can read (`systemctl edit` writes mode 0644), so the installer warns about it, as about `GOBLIN_SECRET_KEY` in `/etc/uptime-goblin.env`: load the key from a file only root can read, or use `SetCredentialEncrypted=`. In `/var/lib/uptime-goblin`, which the `uptime-goblin` account controls, the installer reads files only as regular files, never through a symbolic link (its private copy of `control.sqlite` in `/tmp`, to check the key, is at most 1 GiB, also when the file grows while it is copied, and is removed before the key it copies out of the data directory is written there; the unit's text, which holds the key when a drop-in sets it with `SetCredential=`, is removed from there before the copy is made), and copies to `/etc/uptime-goblin/secret.key` the key it checked, so the service account cannot have it copy a file only root may read; a link there is refused, changing nothing. A key file that `/etc/uptime-goblin.env` or a credential drop-in names in `/var/lib/uptime-goblin` (`GOBLIN_SECRET_KEY_FILE`, `GOBLIN_SECRET_KEY_PREVIOUS_FILE`, `LoadCredential=`, also through a link into it) is refused before it is opened, changing nothing, with the way out (for `/var/lib/uptime-goblin/secret.key`: remove the line, and the installer copies that key to `/etc/uptime-goblin/secret.key`), since root would read such a file through any link the `uptime-goblin` account made, and systemd reads a credential as root at every start. No key file is waited on: one that is not a regular file (a FIFO, say) is refused at once. Uptime Goblin never tells you to delete a `secret.key` in the data directory that it reads itself (`GOBLIN_SECRET_KEY_FILE` naming it, or a Compose secret mounted from it), since that file is the key it reads: it warns that the key travels with every copy of the data directory and to copy it out first; and beside a systemd credential or Compose secret holding the same key, it says to delete the data directory's copy only once the credential or secret no longer comes from it. A start refused because its key does not open the data (`key_mismatch`) names a `secret.key` in the data directory that another source outranked, with its fingerprint and whether it opens the data (a volume 1.5 ran on without `compose.secret.yaml` keeps it, also through a rollback to 1.4.x). Uptime Goblin opens its key file as the `uptime-goblin` account, so before it stops the service the installer gives `/etc/uptime-goblin/secret.key` back to `root:uptime-goblin`, mode 0440, when a restore left it root's alone (Uptime Goblin would refuse to start, exit status 78), and refuses, changing nothing, any other key file Uptime Goblin opens that the `uptime-goblin` account cannot read, with the `chown` and `chmod` that fix it.

- **Wall displays (new).** `goblin-admin display code --name <name>` prints a one-time code, valid for 10 minutes, that signs a wall display in on its `?kiosk=1` page. Its session can read the dashboard and nothing else: no configuration, no Add source, pause or Refresh, no Account page, no way to sign other sessions out or to change the password, and the dashboard it reads leaves out source addresses, collector settings, error texts, stored alert messages and sources hidden from the dashboard. It lasts up to 30 days and ends after 7 days without a request (`GOBLIN_DISPLAY_SESSION_DAYS`, `GOBLIN_DISPLAY_IDLE_DAYS`); every request it makes counts. - **A full disk.** Uptime Goblin keeps 8 MiB in `run/space-reserve` and releases them when the disk fills, so sign-in and alert delivery keep working; settings changes answer 503 `storage_full` (the form keeps your changes), readings pause, a notice goes out (Uptime Goblin's own notices follow **Evaluate alerts**, **Notify when an alert begins** and **Notify when a source recovers**, as alerts do), and everything returns once space is freed. Uptime Goblin starts on a full disk too. A disk-full or history notice sent before a stop is followed by its recovery after the next start (while **Notify when a source recovers** is on) when the condition ended meanwhile (a clean-up and a restart, a reboot, a rollback to 1.4.x and an upgrade again), also when the notice itself was still waiting to be retried; a start on a disk still full sends no second notice.

The `goblin-admin` key commands print none: start Uptime Goblin after one (after `key remove-data-copy`, which runs beside a running Uptime Goblin, sign in once), since its entry can be removed unseen until Uptime Goblin's next entry covers it. - **Docker builds leave your own files out of the image (security fix).** `.dockerignore` allowed the directories `lib/`, `public/`, `docs/` and `deploy/` before naming the files to ship, and allowing a directory allows everything below it. So a file kept there in the checkout (a copy of `auth.json`, a `.env` backup) was sent to the Docker builder, one under `lib/`, `public/` or `docs/` was copied into the image, and on the 1.5 branch, whose web app files need no sign-in, one under `public/` (unless its name starts with a dot) was served to anyone who could reach Uptime Goblin. - **The Docker tests scan whole layers, and remove only what they made.** The layer scan reads plain, gzip or zstd layers and follows each layer's tar headers and the file sizes they give, including a size given in a pax header (as for a file of 8 GiB or more), and fails unless the layer ends with tar's two end-of-archive headers, where a header is due: zero bytes at the end are not enough, since a file that ends in zeros can end a layer cut short before its next file, and the files after the cut would go unscanned. It also fails on header forms that GNU tar and bsdtar were found to read differently, since one of them could then stop early: a header whose checksum does not match (bsdtar skips it without failing); a global pax header; a size on a link, directory, device or FIFO; data in a file named like a directory; a sparse file, or old GNU sparse fields; a size field holding more than digits and padding; an unknown `ustar` magic; extra blanks after a pax record's length; or another entry type. It also fails unless tar extracts exactly the file data those headers frame: bsdtar (macOS) read a file whose name starts with `._` as Mac metadata for the next file and left its content out without failing, so the scan missed what such a file held; the scan now has bsdtar read such a file like any other. Where tar has read the end-of-archive headers it may stop reading and exit with the rest of the layer, such as padding, still to be written to it; the scan then failed on a valid layer now and then ("write EPIPE" or "Premature close"). It now reads and checks the rest of the layer itself, and still fails unless tar exits 0 having extracted exactly that file data. After each Docker test, its clean-up removes the containers whose ids its own `docker create` returned (recorded before they start, so one that fails to start goes too), then its Compose projects by their own names and files, its volumes, and last its image, checks that each is gone and fails, naming any that is not. Before, a test removed its image first, which only untags an image a container still uses, so the image stayed behind. Nothing is chosen by image: that would also take another test's container, or one of yours, whose image has the same id. Every container these tests run has memory, CPU and process limits. A key-recipe test no longer fails when Docker kills a `goblin-admin` run together with the restarting container it ran in (exit 137): such a run is not counted, and the volume's key is still checked after it. The key test that restarts Uptime Goblin with `docker compose restart`, which keeps the container and its earlier log, now reads only that start's log, from the start time Docker records: it read from a second before the restart by the host's clock, and failed now and then on a warning the earlier start had logged within that second.

- **The memory budget sees a limit set on a parent cgroup.** With no `GOBLIN_COLLECTION_MEMORY_MB`, the budget for memory-hungry work is a quarter of the memory limit, or of total memory when there is none. The limit came only from Node's `process.constrainedMemory()`, which reads the process's own cgroup, so a process in a child cgroup with no limit of its own missed the limit of the LXC or container around it. Uptime Goblin's systemd unit runs in such a child cgroup (`system.slice/uptime-goblin.service`), so in a 1 GiB LXC or container where total memory shows the whole machine's, the budget was a quarter of the machine's memory: about 3 GiB on a 12 GB machine, instead of 256 MiB. The budget now takes the lowest limit of the process's own cgroup and every parent it can see (cgroup v2 or v1). Measured in Docker on Node 24: 1 GiB under a 4 GiB limit and 256 MiB under 1 GiB, as before; 256 MiB for a child cgroup inside a 1 GiB container (about 3 GiB before); a quarter of total memory under a limit above it, and on macOS, which has no cgroups. The budget module is not yet used by the server in this pre-release.

That second open never waits: a FIFO or other special file put in the file's place counts as such a change at once, rather than leaving the command waiting for a writer. A file that `GOBLIN_SECRET_KEY_PREVIOUS_FILE` names and that cannot be found or read is named the same way; in both cases the command had said that `<data>/secret.key` changed, which it had not. A command run after such a deletion was not affected: it finds no Compose secret and keeps the copy as the key in use.

With the setting it starts and says at every start which limits it runs without; `/api/system` and **Configure → System** show what the start found, read-only (no page or API sets it), and the audit log's start entry records the limits it runs without (`unlimited`; its coming and going is a security change). The numeric limits are unchanged. Docker Compose's `restart: unless-stopped` retries a refusal, unlike the unit's `RestartPreventExitStatus=78`. After a failed start the native installer shows the service status and the recent log, then rolls back, as after any failed start: it does not infer from the journal which start refused or why, and recommends no `GOBLIN_ALLOW_UNLIMITED` of its own, since a log line may be an earlier start's (the refusal's own lines name each limit and how to apply it). Not yet measured on real Docker, LXC, rootless, cgroup v1, one-CPU or NAS hosts. `goblin-admin` one-off commands are not checked by this change.
- **The limits check cannot be turned off from the environment (security).** The systemd unit's `ExecStart=` and the image's `CMD` start Uptime Goblin with `--supervised` (the native installer keeps it when it writes the unit), so `GOBLIN_SUPERVISED=0` in `/etc/uptime-goblin.env`, an `-e` option of `docker run` or a Compose override no longer turns supervision off: it is ignored, with a line at the start. A value of `GOBLIN_SUPERVISED` that is neither yes nor no refuses the start (it meant off, with a warning), and so does any argument to `server.mjs` other than one `--supervised`. The native installer checks `GOBLIN_ALLOW_UNLIMITED` and `GOBLIN_SUPERVISED` in `/etc/uptime-goblin.env` by this release's rules before an upgrade changes anything.

**Upgrade notes (1.4.x → 1.5):**

- **Supervised installs need their memory, CPU and process limits in force (breaking).** Check before upgrading with the commands in [resource limits](docs/installation.md#resource-limits): a limit that reads `max`, or a file that is missing, makes 1.5 refuse to start until you apply it or name it in `GOBLIN_ALLOW_UNLIMITED` (`/etc/uptime-goblin.env`, or `.env` for Docker Compose). On a host whose kernel has no CPU bandwidth control, the `compose.override.yaml` needs `cpus: 0` and `cpuset: '0'` (or `GOBLIN_ALLOW_UNLIMITED=cpu` on a host with one CPU). Run `grep GOBLIN_SUPERVISED /etc/uptime-goblin.env` before upgrading: a `GOBLIN_SUPERVISED=0` there no longer turns the check off, and a value that is neither yes nor no refuses the start. Under Docker Compose a refused start repeats until you stop the service (`docker compose stop`).
- **Check `GOBLIN_TRUST_PROXY` first.** 1.5 refuses to start (exit status 78) with a value that 1.4.3 accepted but that has an entry with extra slash segments, such as `192.0.2.0/24/extra`, an entry that trusts a range wider than /8 for IPv4 or /32 for IPv6, such as `0.0.0.0/0` or `::/0`, the NAT64 prefix `64:ff9b::/96` (or the local-use `64:ff9b:1::/96`), the IPv4-translated prefix `::ffff:0:0:0/96`, the IPv4-compatible prefix `::/96` or any range that holds one of them (each is every IPv4 address), more than 64 entries, or ranges that together cover more than two /8 (or /32) ranges, such as `10.0.0.0/8,11.0.0.0/8,12.0.0.0/8` (loopback is not counted; the private ranges and `100.64.0.0/10` together fit): every client in a trusted range could choose its own address and pass plain HTTP off as HTTPS. It refuses a value of only commas and blanks, such as `,`, too, which names no proxy (an empty value or `off` trusts none). The native installer checks the value in `/etc/uptime-goblin.env` (unless `--trust-proxy` replaces it) and as systemd would give it to Uptime Goblin, and a `--trust-proxy` value, before it changes anything, and refuses such an upgrade, changing nothing; it checks `GOBLIN_MAX_CONNECTIONS_PER_CLIENT` the same way. Before upgrading, correct it in `/etc/uptime-goblin.env` (or `.env`) to addresses, or addresses with one prefix length such as `192.0.2.0/24`, naming your proxies as narrowly as you can. Check `HOST`, `PORT` and `GOBLIN_ALLOWED_HOSTS` too: 1.5 refuses a `HOST` that is not an IP address or a host name, a `PORT` that is not a number from 1 to 65535 in digits without a leading zero (`04317`, which 1.4.3 read as 4317, included), and more than 64 `GOBLIN_ALLOWED_HOSTS` entries. The native installer checks `HOST` and `PORT` in `/etc/uptime-goblin.env` before it changes anything and refuses the upgrade, changing nothing, with one of those; more than 64 `GOBLIN_ALLOWED_HOSTS` entries are found only when 1.5 refuses to start, and the upgrade is rolled back. It also refuses, changing nothing, an `/etc/uptime-goblin.env` that systemd would not read (a NUL, or a name or value that is not valid UTF-8) or that continues a setting or a comment onto the next line (a quote still open at the end of a line, or a backslash at the end of one): put each setting on a line of its own. It refuses as well, changing nothing, where a drop-in (`Environment=`, `EnvironmentFile=`, `PassEnvironment=`, `UnsetEnvironment=`) or systemd's own environment would give Uptime Goblin another `HOST` or `PORT` than `/etc/uptime-goblin.env`, and where it cannot tell which they would give: set both there alone.
- **`config.json` must be UTF-8.** A hand-edited `config.json` saved in another encoding (Latin-1, say) loaded under 1.4.x with its other bytes replaced by `�`; 1.5 refuses to start on it (exit status 78, "it is not valid UTF-8 text"), so that a password is never changed silently. Save it as UTF-8 before upgrading. A `config.json` that is a symbolic link must point at a file that exists.
- **Scripts that call the HTTP API:** a body over a route's limit now gets 413 instead of 400; `POST /api/refresh`, `POST /api/auth/logout` and the `DELETE` routes refuse a body other than none or `{}`; sign-in and the password change refuse unknown fields; `If-Match` must be a plain revision; a request needs exactly one `Host` and a path as its target (`curl`, `wget` and browsers send both); a JSON body declared with a `charset` must say UTF-8.
- **Reload open Uptime Goblin tabs once after upgrading.** A 1.4.3 tab without unsaved changes reloads itself into the sign-in page.
- **nginx must send `proxy_set_header X-Forwarded-Proto $scheme;`** (not a fixed `https`) and forward the Host (`proxy_set_header Host $http_host;`). Check before upgrading: after browsing Uptime Goblin through nginx once, `sudo cat /var/lib/uptime-goblin/run/status.json` must show `"forwardedProto": "https"`. The installer refuses the upgrade until it does (or `GOBLIN_INSECURE_HTTP_SESSIONS=1`, which `--allow-insecure-http` adds, or `--proxy-proto-checked`). Restarting 1.4.3 empties the record, so browse after its last start, and keep it running until the installer has read it (stopping it removes the record); from 1.4.2, upgrade to 1.4.3 first or use `--proxy-proto-checked`. Serve Uptime Goblin's host name over HTTPS only: let plain HTTP do nothing but redirect, and add HSTS once HTTPS works ([the nginx example](docs/installation.md#https-trust-and-network-access)).
- **If the sign-in page shows a proxy message** after upgrading: fix nginx; or add `GOBLIN_INSECURE_HTTP_SESSIONS=1` to `/etc/uptime-goblin.env` and restart; or, on a native install that listens on 127.0.0.1 (the default) or on every address (`HOST=0.0.0.0`), sign in through `ssh -L 4317:127.0.0.1:4317 <host>` at `http://localhost:4317`. An install made with `--listen <address>` does not listen on 127.0.0.1, and a tunnel to its address is plain HTTP to Uptime Goblin: there, use `GOBLIN_INSECURE_HTTP_SESSIONS=1` on a trusted network. Under Docker the published port needs plain-HTTP sign-in on: `GOBLIN_INSECURE_HTTP_SESSIONS=1`, or the Compose default while the port is published on loopback. See [when sign-in is refused](docs/authentication.md#when-sign-in-is-refused).
- **Restart Uptime Goblin after changing `GOBLIN_INSECURE_HTTP_SESSIONS`** (or `GOBLIN_LEGACY_BASIC`). Under Docker Compose that is `docker compose up -d`, which reads `.env` again; `docker compose restart` keeps the values the container was created with.
- **Scripts using Basic keep working on upgraded installs; new installs have Basic off.** On the plain-HTTP (`http`) transport (a script that calls `http://<uptime-goblin-host>:4317` across the network, or a proxy that does not send `X-Forwarded-Proto: https`) Basic needs `GOBLIN_INSECURE_HTTP_SESSIONS=1` (or Docker Compose's default on its loopback port), or it gets 403 `https_required`: call Uptime Goblin through its HTTPS address instead, or from the Uptime Goblin computer itself, which needs no opt-in. The exception is a proxy on the Uptime Goblin computer that sends no forwarding headers and keeps a loopback Host (nginx's default `Host: 127.0.0.1:4317` with a bare `proxy_pass`): Uptime Goblin takes its requests for the Uptime Goblin computer's own (the `loopback` transport), so Basic through it is accepted with no opt-in and no warning in the log, also from clients that reach the proxy over plain HTTP (not under Docker Compose, where a proxy on the Docker host reaches Uptime Goblin from the Docker gateway, as plain HTTP). Make such a proxy send `X-Forwarded-For` and `X-Forwarded-Proto` and name it in `GOBLIN_TRUST_PROXY` (`loopback`): over plain HTTP, Basic through it then needs the opt-in like any other. A script that downloads the configuration export (`GET /api/config/export`) with Basic adds `-H 'X-Goblin-Request: 1'` wherever it calls Uptime Goblin from (403 `cross_origin` otherwise). A value of `GOBLIN_LEGACY_BASIC` other than yes or no refuses the start. nginx configurations that inject Basic for browsers stop working: sign in on the page.
- **1.5 test installs: check `/etc/uptime-goblin.env`.** An install made or upgraded with an earlier 1.5 pre-release installer may hold `GOBLIN_INSECURE_HTTP_SESSIONS=1` under the comment "# Added by the Uptime Goblin 1.5 installer: keeps plain-HTTP sign-in working as in 1.4.x. Set it to 0 once Uptime Goblin is served over HTTPS.": that installer added it by itself, and it lets browsers and legacy Basic scripts sign in over plain HTTP. Decide when you upgrade: set it to `0` (`sudoedit /etc/uptime-goblin.env`) to refuse sign-in over plain HTTP (serve Uptime Goblin over HTTPS through a reverse proxy), or, on a network you trust, keep it by running the installer with `--allow-insecure-http`, which replaces that comment with one that names who chose it. Without either, the installer stops every such install, changing nothing, also one on loopback or behind a trusted proxy: while the line says `1`, a browser or script that reaches an Uptime Goblin listening on the network directly, not through the proxy, or through a proxy on the Uptime Goblin host that relays plain HTTP, can still sign in over plain HTTP. To keep the line without the option, remove that comment or put one of your own in its place. A `=1` line without that comment just before it is kept as your decision.
- **A native install that listens on the network without a trusted proxy stops the upgrade** until you choose, changing nothing: put Uptime Goblin behind an HTTPS proxy (`HOST=127.0.0.1` and `--trust-proxy loopback`), run the installer with `--allow-insecure-http` on a network you trust (the password, every script's Basic password and the cookie then cross it in clear), or set `GOBLIN_INSECURE_HTTP_SESSIONS=0` in `/etc/uptime-goblin.env` to keep plain HTTP refused ([HTTPS, cookies and plain HTTP](docs/authentication.md#https-cookies-and-plain-http)).
- **Docker Compose with `GOBLIN_BIND_ADDRESS` set to a LAN address (or `0.0.0.0`): choose before upgrading (breaking).** While `.env` sets neither `GOBLIN_TRUST_PROXY` nor `GOBLIN_INSECURE_HTTP_SESSIONS`, 1.5 refuses to start there (exit status 78; Compose restarts it, and `docker compose logs uptime-goblin` repeats "Docker Compose publishes Uptime Goblin on … (GOBLIN_BIND_ADDRESS), which is not a loopback address, …"), where 1.4.3 signed browsers and scripts in over plain HTTP. In `.env`, serve Uptime Goblin through an HTTPS proxy and set `GOBLIN_TRUST_PROXY` to the address it connects from (plain-HTTP sign-in is then off), or set `GOBLIN_INSECURE_HTTP_SESSIONS=1` on a network you trust (the password and the cookie cross it in clear, with a warning at every start), or `0`; then `docker compose up -d`. On the default loopback address nothing changes: Compose's default allows plain-HTTP sign-in there, because only the Docker host reaches the port. Set `0` once Uptime Goblin is served over HTTPS (an empty value is off too; removing the line is not). `docker compose config | grep GOBLIN_INSECURE_HTTP_SESSIONS` shows what Compose passes (`compose-default` while `.env` does not set it). Compose's default follows `GOBLIN_BIND_ADDRESS` only, so an override that publishes the port elsewhere must set it as well, one that makes Uptime Goblin reachable without that port (`network_mode: host`, a macvlan or ipvlan network, a network shared with other containers) must set `GOBLIN_INSECURE_HTTP_SESSIONS` itself (`0`, or `1` on a network you trust), and a host firewall such as `ufw` does not filter a port Docker publishes (Docker's `DOCKER-USER` chain does).
- **Docker: keep the 1.4.3 image, and build with `--pull`.** Tag the running image first, `docker image tag orbit-homelab:local orbit-homelab:1.4.3` (the build replaces `orbit-homelab:local`, and the rollback re-tags that name), then build with `docker compose build --pull` ([updates and rollback](docs/installation.md#updates-and-rollback)). 1.4.3's `Dockerfile` named the `node:24-bookworm-slim` tag, so a plain build of it reused whatever image the host held under that name; 1.5's pins the image by digest, so every build of it uses the same one, with `--pull` or without it, and adds Debian's `libpcre2-8-0` security update on top: the build needs Debian's package servers as well as npm's registry.
- **Docker Compose limits the container** to 1 GiB of memory, 1 CPU and 256 processes (1.4.3's `compose.yaml` set none); set other values in `compose.override.yaml` ([Docker Compose](docs/installation.md#docker-compose)). On a host whose kernel has no CPU bandwidth control (some NAS systems), `docker compose up` then fails with "NanoCPUs can not be set, as your kernel does not support CPU CFS scheduler or the cgroup is not mounted": put `cpus: 0` and `cpuset: '0'` in `compose.override.yaml` there, a CPU set in place of the CPU time limit ([troubleshooting](docs/troubleshooting.md); `cpus: 0` alone now leaves Uptime Goblin no CPU limit, and it refuses to start). Compose reads that file by itself only for a command given no `-f` while `.env` sets no `COMPOSE_FILE`, so on such a host name it last in `COMPOSE_FILE` with `compose.secret.yaml` (`COMPOSE_FILE=compose.yaml:compose.secret.yaml:compose.override.yaml`, also in place of the line each key recipe writes), add `-f compose.override.yaml` after `-f compose.yaml` in the key check, the copy and `MOVE` recipes and the key reset, and leave `--cpus 1` out of the `docker run` line that restores a snapshot: as written, each of them gives Docker `cpus: 1`.
- **Wall displays sign in with a display code.** A wall display that kept its 1.4.x Basic login has to sign in again after the upgrade: create a one-time code on the Uptime Goblin host with `goblin-admin display code --name <name>` and type it on the display's `?kiosk=1` page ([wall displays](docs/authentication.md#wall-displays)). The display then reads only the dashboard, and stays signed in for up to 30 days while it refreshes. A `?kiosk=1` page signed in with the admin password before the upgrade keeps every right the admin has and is signed out after 12 hours, since its refreshes do not count as use; a banner on it offers **Set up**, and once signed out it asks for a code, never the password. Before moving displays over, change the admin password if people you do not fully trust could use one, and delete Uptime Goblin's saved login from each display browser ([the steps](docs/authentication.md#wall-displays)).
- **Free space:** the first start takes a pinned snapshot of `config.json` and `auth.json` (the data as 1.4.x left it; `control.sqlite` does not exist yet, and `history.sqlite` is left out: 1.5 only adds three columns and a partial index to its `events` table, which 1.4.x ignores), then creates `control.sqlite`; it needs about 16 MiB free plus their size.
- **Rolling back to 1.4.3 or 1.4.2 uses the current password** and needs no preparation of the data ([rolling back 1.5 to 1.4.3](docs/installation.md#rolling-back-15-to-143)), unless Uptime Goblin's pages show "Uptime Goblin saved the admin password but could not update auth.json": until that banner has gone, `auth.json` still holds the previous password, and 1.4.x signs in with it, so fix the disk and wait for the banner to go (or restart 1.5, which tries again at once) before you roll back; webhook receivers that check signatures must accept unsigned requests first (below). To go back to the data of before the upgrade too, restore the pinned snapshot with the 1.5 code still installed: 1.4.x has no `goblin-admin`. That removes `control.sqlite`, as restoring any copy of the data from before the upgrade (a deploy tarball) does: the waiting notifications and the delivery history go with it, and so does the audit log (the next start begins a new, empty one, which lists no restore: export it first under **Configure → Audit log**), and 1.5 makes a new webhook signing key when it runs again, whose public key receivers need before they check signatures again; `restore-snapshot` says so. After a code rollback run the kept copy, `sudo -u uptime-goblin env GOBLIN_SECRET_KEY_FILE=/etc/uptime-goblin/secret.key node /opt/uptime-goblin.previous/lib/cli/goblin-admin.mjs restore-snapshot SNAPSHOT_ID --data-dir /var/lib/uptime-goblin` (with `SNAPSHOT_ID` replaced by the snapshot's id, which `goblin-admin snapshots` lists), or under Compose the kept 1.5 image ([goblin-admin](docs/installation.md#goblin-admin)).
- **Every reverse proxy on the Uptime Goblin host must send forwarding headers** (`X-Forwarded-For`, `X-Forwarded-Proto`): through one that sends none, remote clients share the ssh tunnel's sign-in lockout.
- **Alerts are now saved before they are sent**, retried (30 s rising to 15 min, for up to 24 hours) and kept in **Configure → Notifications**; a restart or upgrade no longer loses them. A recovery waits until its alert notification went out on that channel. A notification is not sent if its source was deleted or alerts of its kind were switched off while it waited. Nothing 1.4.x sent is sent again.
- **Sources and mail servers behind NAT64 or 6to4:** one reached through an IPv6 address that carries a link-local, multicast or reserved IPv4 address stops collecting or sending after the upgrade, like one at that IPv4 address itself; one whose address carries a loopback address needs `GOBLIN_ALLOW_LOOPBACK=1`.
- **Webhook receivers:** the body is unchanged. Requests now send `User-Agent: Uptime-Goblin` and carry `X-Goblin-Delivery` (the same for every attempt of one notification: drop repeats with it) and `X-Goblin-Signature` (Ed25519 over the delivery id, the time and the body; verify it with the public key under **Configure → Notifications → Show public key**). A request the receiver does not answer within 8 seconds is sent once more after 2 minutes. Link-local, multicast and reserved addresses are refused. A redirect or a 401/403/404/410 answer, or an untrusted certificate, pauses the webhook: Uptime Goblin tries one queued alert again every 15 minutes and at each start, and you can press **Resume**; queued alerts wait meanwhile, and email tells you the webhook is paused.
- **Email:** a sign-in failure pauses email until you save email settings, send a successful test email, or press **Resume** (and Uptime Goblin tries once at each start). A message whose acceptance is unknown is sent once more after 5 minutes with the same `Message-ID`.
- **Encryption key.** Native installs: the installer creates `/etc/uptime-goblin/secret.key` (or keeps the key you have, after checking it opens your data) and sets `GOBLIN_SECRET_KEY_FILE`; **back it up separately from `/var/lib/uptime-goblin`**: a backup of the data cannot be started without it. Restore it where the `uptime-goblin` account can read it: `sudo install -o root -g uptime-goblin -m 0440 KEY_BACKUP_FILE /etc/uptime-goblin/secret.key`, with `KEY_BACKUP_FILE` replaced by the path of your backup (in `/etc/uptime-goblin`, `root:uptime-goblin`, mode 0750). Docker: use `compose.secret.yaml` (what the volume holds decides its key, not the release running, and the file gives the check: data from 1.4.x has no key yet, so the upgrade from 1.4.x makes a new one, as a new install does; a volume that 1.5 already ran on without the override copies its `/var/lib/uptime-goblin/secret.key` into it first, also when it runs 1.4.x again after a rollback, which leaves that file alone; one that 1.5 ran on with the override keeps the `orbit_secret_key` it has, for which the check prints `KEEP`, and the new-key line never replaces an existing file, and makes the key under `umask 077`, so the file is private from its first byte; the check runs `goblin-admin key compose-check` in the 1.5 image and tries each key there is on the data, so a volume that still holds an older `secret.key` beside a working `orbit_secret_key` (after `key rewrap`) says `KEEP`, not `COPY`, `MOVE` and `RESTORE` name a key file that does not open the data, and the copy recipe's first line never replaces an existing `orbit_secret_key` either; the copy and `MOVE` recipes read `/var/lib/uptime-goblin/secret.key` with a container of their own and have `goblin-admin` check that it opens the data before they change a file (not with `docker compose cp`, which reads through Uptime Goblin's container and fails once the `orbit_secret_key` a refused container mounted is set aside); their last line deletes the volume's copy with `goblin-admin key remove-data-copy`, which runs in Uptime Goblin's container beside the running Uptime Goblin and deletes it only while Uptime Goblin's key comes from another file that holds the same key and opens the data, and otherwise says why and deletes nothing (never `rm`: pasted whole, a recipe runs every line, also after a step before it stopped); the recipes' command blocks carry no comments, since a zsh without `interactivecomments` (its default) runs a trailing `# …` as more words, and the Linux-only `chown` is a block of its own; on a Linux host each recipe gives the file to uid 1000 with `sudo chown 1000 orbit_secret_key` before the first start, without which every start exits 78, EACCES) and name it in `.env` with `COMPOSE_FILE=compose.yaml:compose.secret.yaml`, so every later `docker compose` command (updates, rollbacks, settings changes, `goblin-admin`) keeps the key: one run with `compose.yaml` alone recreates the container without it, and once the volume's copy is deleted every start exits 78; or accept the generated `/var/lib/uptime-goblin/secret.key` (a warning at each start says the key travels with the volume). On a Linux host without user-namespace remapping, uid 1000 is a host uid too, whose account can then read the key file, and Uptime Goblin's processes (Docker's init and Node, which hold no capabilities) run as it, with gid 1000: while Uptime Goblin runs, any process of host uid 1000 that runs with gid 1000 too (as the processes of a login whose own group is gid 1000 do) can read the mounted key through `/proc/<pid>/root/run/secrets/orbit_secret_key` and the `GOBLIN_PASSWORD` Uptime Goblin started with through `/proc/<pid>/environ`, and read and change Uptime Goblin's data volume through `/proc/<pid>/root/var/lib/uptime-goblin` (`/app/data` before 1.5) (measured with Docker 29.4.0 on OrbStack). A Compose project directory only root can enter keeps only the files from being opened by their paths; what keeps the key from that account is a host where uid 1000 is no one's, or Docker's `userns-remap` (not measured with these recipes, whose `chown` must then name the remapped uid); root, and every account that can use Docker, can read it in any case. With such a directory, run the recipes, and every `docker compose` command, as root there ([the encryption key](docs/installation.md#encryption-key)). A `secret.key` left in the data directory beside another source is reported at each start with the line that deletes it, natively and under Compose: `goblin-admin key remove-data-copy` for a copy of the key Uptime Goblin uses (the native installer's message after it copies that key to `/etc/uptime-goblin/secret.key` gives the same line), and `goblin-admin key remove-data-copy --unused` for an older key, which Uptime Goblin does not use, as after `key rewrap`: it deletes the file only while the key Uptime Goblin is given opens every data key and that file opens none (`key remove-data-copy` keeps such a key, since it is no copy), and neither deletes a file `GOBLIN_SECRET_KEY_PREVIOUS_FILE` names; after `KEEP`, the check prints that line too, and `compose.secret.yaml` says to keep `orbit_secret_key`. `GOBLIN_SECRET_KEY` works but stays visible in `/proc/<pid>/environ`. Docker Compose now gives Uptime Goblin 40 seconds to stop. See [the encryption key](docs/installation.md#encryption-key).
- **A full disk:** Uptime Goblin keeps 8 MiB in `run/space-reserve`; when the disk fills it releases them for sign-in and alert delivery, refuses settings changes (503), pauses saving readings and sends a notice, until space is freed. It starts on a full disk too. File-level backups that include `run/` grow by 8 MiB.
- **Audit log:** Uptime Goblin now keeps an audit log of sign-ins, sign-outs, session and password changes, every settings and source change (with what changed; passwords and keys only as "changed"), source and email tests, refreshes, delivery **Retry** and **Resume**, the webhook signing key's creation and replacement, exports, `goblin-admin` key commands, restores and snapshots, Uptime Goblin's own starts and upgrades, and changes to its security settings: **Configure → Audit log**. Entries are kept 90 days by default (`GOBLIN_AUDIT_RETENTION_DAYS`, 30–3650), at most 250,000. Docker Compose passes both audit settings from `.env`.
- **If Uptime Goblin cannot write its audit log**, settings and source changes, **Retry**, **Resume** and both exports are refused until the disk is fixed, with "Uptime Goblin cannot write its audit log, so nothing was changed. Try again in a moment; if this continues, free up space or increase the disk size of the Uptime Goblin host." (503 `audit_unavailable`). Signing in fails for the same cause. Source tests, test emails and refreshes still run, and their entries then go to Uptime Goblin's log.
- **Keep Uptime Goblin's log.** Uptime Goblin prints a line starting `{"orbit":"audit.checkpoint"` to its log each time it records something. Keep the journal: `goblin-admin verify-audit --checkpoints <export>` (or `--checkpoints -` from a pipe) compares the log with it. Under Docker Compose the container log holds 10 MB and is discarded when the container is recreated: export it before each `docker compose up -d` ([keeping Uptime Goblin's log](docs/installation.md#keeping-uptime-goblins-log)). `GOBLIN_AUDIT_LOG=stdout` copies every entry to the log as well.
- **Rolling back the audit log** to an earlier 1.5 build or to 1.4.x needs no preparation; nothing is audited while it runs, and the next start of this release records the 1.5 builds that opened the data and what changed (a 1.4.x run that changed neither the settings nor the password leaves no trace). After a rollback to an earlier 1.5 build, its `goblin-admin check-data` reports the four audit tables as unregistered; that is expected. The webhook signing key is another matter: never start data that this release has run on with an earlier 1.5 pre-release build, which shows no signing secret there and sends webhooks unsigned (on data that 1.5 pre-release builds before this release left, such a build took the private signing key for its HMAC secret, signed with its text and showed it under **Show signing secret**; if one has run, see [rolling back to 1.4.x](docs/notifications.md#rolling-back-to-14x-and-upgrading-again)). The native installer's automatic rollback never starts such a build that signs with an HMAC secret, with or without a webhook (a failed start may stop before it moves a key out of the field such a build reads, and a key stays in the data without a webhook): it stops the service, keeps this release in `/opt/uptime-goblin` and the earlier build in `/opt/uptime-goblin.previous`, and says how to go on ([verification and automatic rollback](docs/installation.md#verification-and-automatic-rollback)). It does restore and start a 1.5 pre-release build that already signs with Ed25519 (its `lib/` declares this release's public-key route and not the reveal route), as any earlier release: if the failed start of this release got as far as saving the signing settings in its form, that build reads them read-only, answers **Show public key** with 503 `signing_unavailable` and sends webhooks unsigned (its log says "The webhook signing key could not be saved (…); alerts are sent unsigned until it can be."; the installer does not), so receivers that check signatures refuse them, a `401` or `403` pausing the webhook and a `400` or `422` ending each alert as **Failed**, until this release runs again or the data from before the upgrade is restored.
- **Rolling back to 1.4.3 or 1.4.2 needs no preparation of the data, but webhook receivers that check signatures must first accept unsigned requests:** 1.4.x sends webhooks without `X-Goblin-Delivery` and `X-Goblin-Signature` and never retries one, so such a receiver loses every alert for as long as the rollback lasts; turn the check back on after upgrading again (with the new signing key's public key if you restored the data from before the upgrade). The private signing key stays in `control.sqlite`, which 1.4.x ignores, so 1.4.x neither uses nor shows it. Do not start data that this release has run on with an earlier 1.5 pre-release build: it shows no signing secret, cannot make one and sends webhooks unsigned, because this release keeps the private keys in settings fields those builds never open and saves those settings in a form they can read but not change. 1.5 pre-release builds that already signed with Ed25519 kept the private key in the HMAC secret's field, until a start of this release moves it out; an HMAC build started on their data before that took the key for its HMAC secret, signed with its text and showed it under **Show signing secret**: if one has, press **Replace signing key** twice under this release (the second press stops the exposed key signing at once), with `GOBLIN_WEBHOOK_URL` set (set it again first if it was removed: without it the key cannot be replaced, and the exposed key signs again once it is set), and give receivers the new public key. Notifications still waiting when you roll back are not sent by 1.4.x, which sends its own; they are sent when you upgrade again (unless the data from before the upgrade was restored, which removed them) if they are less than 24 hours old and 1.4.x did not handle a later change of the same alert (also when Uptime Goblin was killed while sending one).

Security and reliability fixes reviewed across the browser, saved configuration, history, and installer. This section describes source changes; it is not a deployment record.

Background cleanup stops before its next batch when a longer policy is saved. Already deleted readings cannot be recovered by increasing retention. Storage warnings explain that lowering retention does not free filesystem space by itself.

## 1.4.3 — 2026-09-29

Upgrades you can see: open pages update themselves, the installer proves the new version is the one serving and puts the previous code back when it is not, and Orbit no longer stops without a trace.

**Upgrade notes:** reload each Orbit tab that was opened on 1.4.2 or earlier **once** after upgrading. Those pages contain no update check; from 1.4.3 on, open tabs update themselves. The data directory needs no preparation: `history.sqlite` gains a `latest_notes` table, which 1.4.2 ignores, and Orbit writes `run/status.json` while it runs.

It waits up to 120 seconds (`--verify-timeout`) for proof that 1.4.3 is the process serving. If that fails, it prints the service status and log, moves the new code to `/opt/orbit.failed`, restores the previous code and exits with status 1. After a successful upgrade the previous code stays in `/opt/orbit.previous`.

It now restarts Orbit after any exit that `systemctl stop` did not request, except a refusal to start (exit status 78), sets `ORBIT_SUPERVISED=1`, and starts Node from the path `command -v node` finds (for example `/usr/bin/node`) instead of the versioned file behind it, so upgrading Node no longer leaves the service failing with `203/EXEC`. Re-running the installer repairs a 1.4.2 unit whose Node binary has already gone, and says so.

- **Behind a reverse proxy** with `ORBIT_TRUST_PROXY` set, open Orbit once through the proxy after upgrading, so Orbit records the protocol the proxy forwards (the next release checks it).
- **Verify the release** with `ssh-keygen -Y verify` before installing; see [Verifying a release](docs/installation.md#verifying-a-release).
- **Rolling back to 1.4.2** restores code only; the data directory works on 1.4.2 as it is. Native: stop the service, swap `/opt/orbit` and `/opt/orbit.previous`, start it again ([Rolling back 1.4.3](docs/installation.md#rolling-back-143-to-142)); 1.4.2 runs under the 1.4.3 unit unchanged. Docker: build or re-tag the 1.4.2 image and recreate the container. Open 1.4.3 tabs reload themselves into 1.4.2. On 1.4.2, a time-zone offset such as `UTC+05:30` is replaced by `local` (logged) and stays `local` after upgrading again, so set it again; collector notes disappear until 1.4.3 collects again; `/health` needs sign-in again. After upgrading again, reload tabs opened on 1.4.2 once more.

### Security

- `/health` is public and reveals nothing about your lab: it returns `{"status":"ok"}`, with no version, source names or states, and still requires an allowed Host. `?instance=<value>` adds `"instance":"match"` or `"mismatch"`; the value is compared in constant time and never echoed.
- Signed-in responses carry `X-Orbit-Version` and `X-Orbit-Build`, so open pages can tell that Orbit was updated. The public `/health`, sign-in challenges and refused requests reveal nothing about the release; see [version headers](docs/security.md#public-health-check-and-version-headers).
- A request address that cannot be parsed is answered 400 before sign-in, instead of a logged server error.
- Release archives carry `SHA256SUMS.sig`, an SSH signature over `SHA256SUMS` (namespace `orbit-release`, signer `release@orbit-homelab`). The public key is in `deploy/allowed_signers`, and its fingerprint is published in README.md and SECURITY.md.
- The installer installs exactly the files listed in `deploy/manifest.txt`, checks each of them against `SHA256SUMS` when installing from a release archive, and refuses a Node binary under `/root`, `/home` or `/run/user`. All of these checks run before anything on the host changes.
- Under supervision (`ORBIT_SUPERVISED=1`, set by the systemd unit and the Docker image) Orbit exits 0 only after a stop signal; an exit that no stop signal caused is reported as 1 and restarted. A refusal to start exits 78, and systemd does not restart it every few seconds.
- `Idempotency-Key` results are kept in memory only, for 10 minutes, separately for each sign-in password, and request bodies are compared by a keyed digest, never stored. Password changes are never stored or replayed.
- `run/status.json` is written with mode 600 in a mode 700 directory and removed when Orbit stops cleanly.

### Fixes

- **Open tabs keep up with upgrades** (the report that Orbit "does not auto start after upgrade"). Before, a tab kept running the old page after an upgrade, and could stop updating altogether:
  - Pages and their script and style files are tied to a build, and every signed-in answer names the build Orbit is running. A page from another build reloads itself when nothing would be lost, returning to the same expanded card, range, measurement and scroll position. It waits until 10 seconds after your last key press or click and shows "Orbit was updated. Reloading this page…" first.
  - When reloading would lose something (unsaved edits, a change without an answer yet, an open dialog), a banner offers **Reload** instead, and the page reloads by itself once that is resolved.
  - Tabs in the background check `/api/version` every 5 minutes. Wall displays opened with `?kiosk=1` always reload.
  - At most 2 automatic reloads happen per 10 minutes. If a proxy or browser cache keeps serving the old page, a banner says so instead of reloading again.
  - A request for another build's script or style is answered with the current file and not cached, instead of 404.
- **A page never freezes on a request that gets no answer.** Reads time out after 30 seconds, saves after 60 and connection tests after 150. A dashboard load still running after 45 seconds is restarted, and the page loads again when the network comes back. Meanwhile it shows "Reconnecting…" and recovers by itself.
- **Saves across a restart are never applied twice.** Every save carries an `Idempotency-Key` and the settings revision it was based on. When a save gets no answer, the page retries it once, and only if the settings are provably unchanged; otherwise it says the change "may have been saved", shows the latest settings and keeps your edits. Adding a source now uses the same conflict check as other saves, and a conflict keeps your entries.
- **Orbit no longer stops silently.** The service restarted only after a failure, so an exit with status 0 left it stopped while the installer reported success. The unit now restarts after any unexpected exit, and under supervision an exit that no stop signal caused is reported as a failure. A start path that does not resolve to `server.mjs` is refused with a message and exit status 78.
- **Upgrading Node no longer strands the service.** 1.4.2's installer wrote the versioned Node file into the unit, so a Node upgrade that removed it made the next start fail with `203/EXEC`.
- **Installer:** the 10-second readiness wait is replaced by verification of the new process (version, build, the process systemd started, and a per-start instance check, for up to 120 seconds), with automatic rollback. File lists come from `deploy/manifest.txt` instead of lists in the script, so a new module cannot be left out.
- **Busy saves are bounded.** At most 16 saves wait for their turn, each for at most 20 seconds; beyond that Orbit answers 503 with `Retry-After: 5` and applies nothing. A save whose browser disconnected before its turn is skipped.
- **Lowering retention** holds the save back for at most 20 seconds; pruning then continues in the background.
- **Shutdown** closes connections still open after 10 seconds, so a stalled client cannot hold up a stop.

### Improvements

- **Time zones** accept fixed offsets written `UTC±HH:MM`, such as `UTC+05:30`, `UTC-08:00` or `UTC+05:45` (hours 00–14; minutes 00, 15, 30 or 45). They show correctly in every browser. `UTC+00:00` is saved as `UTC`, and a minus sign (−) as a hyphen; a hand-edited `config.json` is rewritten once, with a log line.
- **Notes about readings left out.** When a limit leaves readings out, the card and the connection test say so in a short note: a Sonarr or Radarr library that is too large; more than 512 measurements; more than 128 Proxmox guests; more than 64 UniFi devices; more than 256 network interfaces; more than 200 filesystems or Windows volumes; CrowdSec alerts over 16 MB or at the 1,000-alert limit; UniFi lists over 20,000 entries.
- **Charts** request only the measurement they show, so opening a card on a large source transfers much less. CSV export still includes every measurement.
- **System page** shows the build and "Open tabs by version": the Orbit pages that checked in during the last 10 minutes, including those from before 1.4.3.
- **API:**
  - `GET /api/version` returns `{version, build}`.
  - `/api/history/:id` accepts `?metric=<key>`, repeatable up to 20 times.
  - `/api/dashboard` includes `services[].notes`, and connection tests return `notes`.
  - `/api/system` includes `build` and `clients`.
  - Writes accept `Idempotency-Key` (replayed for 10 minutes), and `POST /api/services` honours `If-Match`.
- **Exit statuses:** 0 after a stop signal; 78 only when Orbit refuses to start for a reason it checked itself (invalid settings or `auth.json`, password rules, an invalid `ORBIT_TRUST_PROXY`, a privileged port); 1 for everything else, which is retried: a full, read-only or failing disk, a database error, an address in use or not yet available, or anything unexpected. A full data disk therefore never leaves Orbit stopped for good.
- **Readiness check** (`lib/healthcheck.mjs`) gains `--wait`, `--timeout`, `--instance`, `--data-dir`, `--expect-version`, `--expect-build`, `--unit`, `--pid` and `--print-build`; unknown options exit 2.
- **Docker:** the image runs with `ORBIT_SUPERVISED=1`, its health check allows a 60-second start period, it includes `deploy/manifest.txt` and `deploy/allowed_signers`, and the build fails when a file listed in the manifest did not reach the image.
- **For contributors:**
  - `npm run check` follows every import from the manifest, syntax-checks each module and runs `bash -n` on the installer.
  - `npm test` includes subdirectories; `test:fast`, `test:browser`, `test:upgrade`, `test:installer` and `test:docker` run one suite each, as the five CI jobs do. `TEST_REQUIRE` makes a suite fail instead of skipping when its browser, fixtures, systemd image or Docker daemon is missing.

  - `ORBIT_SIGNING_KEY=<key> npm run package` signs the release.
  - `public/` is read once at startup, so restart Orbit after editing it.

## 1.4.2 — 2026-09-28

Fixes from a full code review of 1.4.1, plus tests for every fix.

**Upgrade notes:** the history database gains an index on first start. This takes a few seconds on a large history and adds about 25 bytes per stored reading (more for long error texts). It is built only when about three times its size is free above the reserve Orbit keeps free (its sort runs in the data directory, not in `/tmp`); otherwise, or if the build fails for any other reason, Orbit starts without it and tries again on a later start. `config.json` gains `revision` and `sourceCounters`; older releases ignore them, and 1.4.1 runs on a 1.4.2 data directory as it is. Rolling back further (to 1.4.0 or earlier) now needs `DROP INDEX IF EXISTS readings_history` before dropping the `error` column; see Backups in docs/installation.md. Settings that newer validation refuses are repaired and saved at the first start, and each repair is logged once:

- time zones other than `local`, `UTC` or a region name are reset to `local`
- trailing `?` or `#` is removed from collection addresses
- credential parameters (such as `?token=` or `#access_token=`) are removed from launch links; the rest of the link is kept

### Security

- Sign-in guessing spread over many addresses is now limited as a whole. After 50 failed password checks within 10 minutes from all clients together, only addresses that used the correct password in the last 30 days can try; others get 429 without a password check, and the pause ends 10 minutes after the failed checks stop. Locked-out addresses are no longer pushed out of the lockout table by failures from other addresses.
- Behind a trusted proxy, only the last `X-Forwarded-For` entry (the one the proxy added) is used, and oversized values are ignored.
- Changing the SMTP server, port, security mode or sender now requires re-entering the app password (or clearing it), so a saved password is never sent to a different server.
- Collection addresses cannot end in a bare `?` or `#`. Launch links cannot carry credential-like query parameters (names containing words such as token, key, secret, password, session or signature, as in `apiToken`, `passkey` or `passhash`), nor fragments made of such key=value pairs (`#access_token=…`). `authuser`, `author`, `keyword`, app routes such as `#!/auth` or `#!/server/…?key=…`, and page anchors are fine.
- New mappings on native Linux and Windows cards cannot reuse the keys of built-in readings (such as `cpu`); saved mappings keep working and are used whenever the host does not report that built-in reading (for example node_exporter on FreeBSD, without MemTotal). Cards using the JSON method are not affected.
- Email: IP-address SMTP hosts work with certificate checks still on. A server that drops the connection before its greeting is tried once, not reconnected until the deadline. The 20-second deadline and cancellation now cover the SMTP address lookup. Cancelling right after a connection opens no longer sends the message.
- The Docker image and release archive set file permissions explicitly, whatever the build machine's umask.

### Fixes

- **Two browsers editing at once:** saves carry the settings revision they started from. A save based on an older copy gets "Settings were changed in another browser or device" instead of overwriting the newer change. Your edits are kept, with a button to load the latest.
- **Saves are applied one at a time**, on the server and within each browser tab, so a save that has to prune a large history cannot interleave with another one, and quick successive changes in one tab no longer get a false conflict.
- **Source IDs are never reused**, so an editor left open for a deleted source cannot change or remove a newer one. (IDs deleted before upgrading were not recorded by older releases and can be used once more.)
- **Charts:**
  - A poll no longer rebuilds the open chart, so focus, dropdowns and the tooltip stay put, also when "Needs attention first" reorders cards or groups.
  - History reloads once the chart's window has moved by one bucket (a 300th of the range, at most once per 15-second poll), when the source's status changes, and right after your own Refresh, pause or edit, instead of on every poll.
  - A chart never shows another range's data.
  - The tooltip reads the point under the pointer.
  - A reading between two failures is drawn.
  - A pause marker no longer hides a real failure in the same bucket.
- **History:**
  - Reads use a covering index: about 2–3 times faster for 30-day charts.
  - Lowering retention or removing a large source deletes in short steps, so collection is not blocked. On a 1.2-million-row history the longest pause went from 6.7 s to 17 ms.
  - The size cap follows free disk space every minute instead of hourly.
  - Pruning runs at startup and then hourly.
- **Sonarr and Radarr:** the library is streamed and only counted, so libraries up to 64 MiB (about 10,000 movies) work. Before, the limit was 16 MB (about 2,500 items). Above the limit, only the library counts are left out; queue, health and disk readings continue. Sonarr's missing count comes from its Wanted view.
- **Collectors:**
  - Connections dropped mid-response are retried as interruptions. A malformed response (broken chunked framing, a corrupt or mislabelled compressed body) is reported as such and not retried.
  - A response that has already arrived beats an expired timer.
  - DNS timeouts say so.
  - UTF-8 byte order marks are accepted.
  - Prometheus scrapes ask for the text format.
  - Proxmox cluster CPU and memory use online nodes only.
  - Windows CPU counts idle, user and privileged time only.
  - Linux uptime uses the host's clock.
  - Plex remuxes count as direct streams (new `direct_stream` reading), not transcodes.
  - CrowdSec counts local decisions and alerts only, not the community blocklist. A very large alert, such as a bulk `cscli decisions import`, is counted without being read into memory, and an oversized alerts list no longer drops the decision and ban readings.
  - UniFi per-device reads share one time limit.
  - Cosmos sample age uses the Cosmos host's clock.
  - Long Linux mount and Windows volume names get stable keys.
- **Scheduling** uses a monotonic clock, so changing the system time no longer skips or bunches polls.
- **Storage:** the all-clear for history write failures waits until every source that failed has written again and five quiet minutes have passed.
- **Editing:**
  - Enter in the rule builder adds the rule, and an unadded rule blocks saving.
  - A blank duration is rejected instead of becoming 0 seconds.
  - Escape and Back respect "keep my changes".
  - Editing a source removed elsewhere explains what happened.
  - Website and bookmark cards describe their real collector.
  - Advanced fields that a source ignores are hidden.
  - A time zone this browser does not support falls back to local time instead of blanking the page.
- **Shutdown:**
  - Requests arriving while Orbit stops get 503.
  - Alerts batched just before a stop are still sent, within a bounded wait.
  - A second stop signal waits for the first.
- **Startup:** Orbit and its readiness check start when launched through a symlinked path; before, they exited silently.
- **API:**
  - A wrong method on a known route returns 405 with `Allow`.
  - The JSON content-type check ignores letter case.
  - CSV exports start with a UTF-8 byte order mark so spreadsheets read them correctly.
- **Docs:** corrected the 1.4.0 note on JSON limits. There is one 750,000-value budget per collection attempt; the 1,000,000-value per-response limit was never reachable.

## 1.4.1 — 2026-09-28

Fewer false and repeated alerts, based on four days of history from a live install.

**Rollback note:** 1.4.1 adds an `error` column to the history database. Earlier releases cannot write to it. Take a backup before upgrading (see Backups in docs/installation.md). To roll back, restore that backup, or stop Orbit and run `node -e "const {DatabaseSync}=require('node:sqlite');const d=new DatabaseSync('/var/lib/orbit/history.sqlite');d.exec('ALTER TABLE readings DROP COLUMN error');d.close()"` (adjust the path to your data directory) before starting the older version.

### Fixes

- Sessions (UniFi local, AMP, Nginx Proxy Manager, CrowdSec) were discarded on any error, so every retry of a timed-out poll signed in again. A session due for renewal keeps being used while it is still valid if renewing fails. AMP sessions rejected inside a normal response are renewed. UniFi's LoginRequired answer is treated as an expired session.
- Requests no longer stop after a fixed 12 seconds: each may use a quarter of the collection deadline (12–25 s), so slow but working sources are not failed.
- Sonarr, Radarr and Prowlarr `health_issues` counts warnings and errors only; informational notices are reported separately as `health_notices`.

### Improvements

- Connection alerts say why the check failed, recoveries say how long the outage lasted, and every failed reading keeps its reason. Hovering a failed point on a chart shows it, and CSV exports have an `error` column.
- Email alerts are gathered until a minute passes without a new one (at most three minutes) and sent as one message listing each source, so a DNS or network outage produces one email instead of one per source. Alerts pending at shutdown are still sent. Webhooks are unchanged.
- Pause, resume and reconfigure markers show as gaps ("No collection"), not failed collections, on charts and in CSV exports.
- Retries after a failed DNS lookup ask DNS again instead of reusing the cached failure.
- Documentation on reducing alert noise and on DNS setups that make every hostname fail at once.


## 1.4.0 — 2026-09-27

### Upgrade notes

- Behind a reverse proxy, set `ORBIT_TRUST_PROXY` (`loopback` for a proxy on the same host, otherwise the proxy's address or CIDR); native installs can re-run the installer with `--trust-proxy`. Without it, one client's failed sign-ins lock out everyone.
- Sources on loopback addresses need `ORBIT_ALLOW_LOOPBACK=1` (installer: `--allow-loopback`).
- While no password has been saved in the app, `ORBIT_PASSWORD` must be 12 to 1024 characters. Orbit also refuses to run without a password when configured for proxy access.
- Sonarr and Radarr disk measurements are now keyed by path (`disk_free_<path>`, `disk_total_<path>`) instead of list position (`disk_0_free`). Update rules that used the old keys; history under the old keys stays but no longer grows.
- Radarr `missing` now counts only monitored, released movies without a file, matching Radarr's Missing view.

### Security

- Throttle failed sign-ins per client with an exponential lockout (5 free failures, then 1 s doubling to 15 min). Add `ORBIT_TRUST_PROXY` to key the lockout on the forwarded client address behind a reverse proxy.
- Verify the bootstrap `ORBIT_PASSWORD` with the same salted scrypt hash as saved passwords. It was previously checked with a fast HMAC, which allowed guessing at network speed.
- Refuse to send a saved source credential to a different server. Changing a source's collection origin now requires re-entering or clearing its credential, which matches the existing SMTP rule.
- Refuse collection from link-local (including cloud metadata), multicast, unspecified and reserved addresses, and from loopback unless `ORBIT_ALLOW_LOOPBACK=1`. Hostnames are resolved and checked inside the request deadline. Penetration test finding 3.3.
- Require the current password to change the Orbit password. A wrong current password counts toward the sign-in lockout, and a cached session no longer clears failed attempts. Finding 3.6.
- Reject unknown fields in settings, source and connection-test requests instead of silently ignoring them. Saved configuration files are not affected. Finding 3.7.
- Remove the Node.js version, platform and architecture from `/api/system` and the System page. Finding 3.4.
- URL-encode UniFi site and device identifiers from controller responses before using them in request paths.
- Add `Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy`, `Cross-Origin-Resource-Policy` and `Permissions-Policy` headers, and additional systemd sandboxing in `deploy/orbit.service`.
- Throttle IPv6 clients per /64 and IPv4-mapped addresses as IPv4. Password checks wait briefly in a bounded queue instead of being refused at once, and attempts refused because sign-in is busy count as failures, so they cannot be used to test guesses for free.
- `ORBIT_TRUST_PROXY` now names the trusted proxy (`loopback`, addresses or CIDRs); `X-Forwarded-For` is ignored from any other peer, so clients cannot pick their own throttling key.
- A successful password change or fresh sign-in clears the failure count, and devices still using a password replaced in the last day are refused without counting as guesses. A held password-change request is re-checked against the lockout after its body arrives.
- Refuse to run without a password when a public hostname is allowed or a proxy is trusted, and refuse proxied requests to a passwordless instance.
- Website checks and bookmarks drop saved credentials when their address moves to another server (they never send them); other sources still require re-entering them.
- JSON mapping paths no longer index into strings or inherited properties, so a session cannot read a saved API key back one character at a time.
- Prometheus parsing is linear time; a hostile exporter could previously block Orbit for seconds per poll.
- JSON responses are limited by structure (1,000,000 values, 64 levels of nesting, counted outside strings), and each collection attempt may read at most 32 MiB and 750,000 values across all its requests and pages, so one hostile source cannot exhaust memory or starve other sources.
- The SMTP server follows the same outbound destination policy as collectors.
- Docker image application files are root-owned and read-only to the runtime user.

### Fixes

- Pausing a source could record two collection-gap markers, which made a lifecycle test flaky.
- Handle static-file stream errors instead of leaving an unhandled stream error.
- Coalesce repeated manual refreshes within 5 seconds.
- Label Sonarr/Radarr disks without a reported path instead of showing `undefined`.
- Availability alerts no longer reset during large outages when many slow sources queue behind each other; only a restart counts as a monitoring gap. History charts and the stale status allow for collection time and queueing.
- A failed history write (for example a full disk) no longer stops alert evaluation and notifications. Orbit sends one storage notification, shows a dashboard notice, keeps 5% of the filesystem (64 MiB to 1 GiB) free by capping the history database, and then recycles the oldest readings. Lowering retention frees space before saving.
- DNS lookups for the destination check are shared and cached briefly, so slow or failing DNS reports "DNS lookup failed" promptly instead of timing out, and one bad hostname no longer starves other lookups. The systemd unit and Docker image set `UV_THREADPOOL_SIZE=16`.
- Editing a source's name, group, colour, visibility, notifications or rules no longer interrupts its collection or resets rate baselines.
- Linux and Windows network rates count each physical interface once and survive interfaces appearing and disappearing.
- Proxmox storage readings are no longer dropped by the 512-measurement cap on large clusters; per-guest detail covers the 128 lowest VMIDs.
- Website checks join the check path correctly when the card URL has a query or fragment.
- An HTTPS address pointing at a plain-HTTP port is reported as a scheme mismatch, not a certificate failure.
- Cancelling email delivery (settings change, timeout, shutdown) now closes an SMTP session that is already sending.
- Deleting a source clears its history before its configuration, and new sources never inherit leftover rows.
- The readiness check works when Orbit listens on a specific address, so the installer no longer fails after a good install.
- History ranges such as `constructor` are rejected.
- UI: sign-in lockouts show a clear message instead of "cannot reach its collector"; proxy error pages are no longer reported as invalid metric mappings; hidden editor sections no longer block or fail a save; "Clear saved credentials" keeps credentials typed in the same save and stays visible for website checks and bookmarks; test email works right after saving a normalized SMTP host; saves always show fresh data.
